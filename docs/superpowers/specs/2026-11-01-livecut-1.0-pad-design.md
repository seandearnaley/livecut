# Livecut 1.0: the pad

Date: 2026-11-01. Direction agreed in conversation. After the main points were
confirmed, the owner asked for the rest to be decided here ("build what you
think is best … canonical, modern, idiomatic … with the cut algorithms
underneath"). Calls made in this document rather than by the owner are marked
**(our call)**.

## Why

The [listening audit](../../audit/1.0-listening.md) found the sound engine
passes and the interface fails. The four cut procedures "sounded great", "no
clicks", and "there is a lot of good stuff in there". But Resonance, Fracture
(grain, tape) and the mode selection are "not intuitive at all", and "the XY
version had a bit more of that".

The owner wants a Kaoss-Pad-style instrument: "the really deep, really cool,
really thoughtful algorithm that's part machine sequencing, and then when you
put your finger on it, you get that human kinetic control". Audio quality and
people dancing matter most. IDM producers can go cerebral with it, but "we
can't have chaotic, messy, ugly sounds, muddy sequences".

## Goals

- **One surface you play.** An XY pad and eight programs replace the five
  settings pages as the way into Livecut.
- **The machine does the detail, you steer the character.** Livecut's cut
  algorithms decide exactly where each cut lands, which slice it uses and how
  a roll ends. The pad steers the overall character.
- **Touch engages.** Pressing the pad brings the program in. Lifting takes it
  out cleanly on the beat. Hold keeps it in.
- **Musical everywhere.** Every spot on the pad is on the grid, respects the
  backbeat and sounds good. The edges are wilder, never messy.
- **Learnable.** The same spot in the same place in the bar always sounds the
  same.
- **Axes that move together.** Each axis drives several related things at
  once, such as faster repeats that also climb in pitch. The owner: "where
  there's multiple axes of relationships, they tend to sound good."

## Non-goals (for 1.0)

- Two-finger or multi-touch playing. The owner said "we don't need to design
  for the two-finger".
- Designing for MPE. A pressure Z is a possible later bonus, and nothing
  depends on it.
- Programs you can edit, or saved banks of your own.
- Added latency. The pad is immediate (Live mode). Studio's 500 ms look-ahead
  and the Spectral engine aren't part of the pad.

## Anti-goals

These would make it a failure even if it technically works:

- Randomness you can't steer, or a pad position that sounds different every
  time.
- Mud. Muddy tails or sequences at any position.
- Clicks or level jumps on engage, release or program switch.
- Any wording in the interface that describes the engine rather than the
  sound, such as "Family: next bar; other settings: next block".

## The surface (layout B, chosen by the owner)

- **Top:** Livecut's phrase ruler, full width. It shows the playing program,
  the bar in the phrase, the cut grid and the playhead, plus a pending switch
  ("→ Loops on the beat").
- **Middle:** a wide XY pad. The two axis captions come from the playing
  program. A glow follows the pointer while it's pressed.
- **Bottom:** eight program buttons in a row. One is lit when playing, one
  blinks while armed. Next to them: **Hold**, **Beat / Bar** (when switches
  and releases land) and **Mix**.
- The editor size stays 880 × 724, with the existing Livecut look and feel.
- **Input:** mouse first, a single point **(owner)**. The Z axis is the mouse
  button: press to engage, release to let go.

## Behaviour

**Engage (press).**
- Cutting restarts with the program's mapping for the current position at the
  next unit boundary of that program's grid. This uses the engine's existing
  rule that a phrase starts on the next unit boundary, so entry is always on
  the grid.
- Until that boundary, the live input passes through, which is what the first
  cut of a block plays anyway, so there's no gap.

**Move.**
- Continuous settings (chances, ranges, amounts) take effect from the next
  block, which is the engine's existing rule.
- A change of discrete zone (subdivision, procedure, repeat count) restarts
  cutting at the next unit boundary, like engaging. Zones have hysteresis
  (about 3% of an axis) so dragging along a border can't flicker
  **(our call)**.

**Release.**
- At the next beat boundary (or bar, with Bar selected), the wet signal fades
  to the input over 5 ms.
- Pressing again before then cancels the release.
- With Hold on, releasing doesn't disengage. Turning Hold off with nothing
  pressed disengages at the next boundary.

**Program switch.**
- The pressed program becomes pending and takes over at the next beat (or
  bar). If the pad is engaged, cutting restarts with the new program at that
  boundary.
- If the pad isn't engaged, the switch is immediate, because nothing is
  audible yet.

**Not engaged.** The output is the input, untouched.

**Mix** is the existing Wet control. It blends the input with the program
while engaged.

**Determinism.**
- Each program has a fixed seed **(our call)**.
- Each program's mapping starts from a fixed neutral base, never from the
  session's legacy settings **(our call)**. So a program sounds the same in
  every project, and phrase seeding by bar makes the same spot in the same bar
  repeat exactly.

## Programs

A program is a pure, deterministic function from `(x, y)` to the engine
settings it controls. It is applied over the program's neutral base. It never
allocates, so it can run on the audio thread, and it's unit-testable without
audio.

The axis curves below are starting points. The final values are tuned by ear,
and the owner's verdicts are recorded in the audit document.

| # | Program | X → | Y ↑ | Relationship | Engine | Slice |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cut** | Density: 8ths to 32nds, with more repeats | Departure: near the original order up to a full re-edit (stutter up, straight down, activity up) | Bottom-left is nearly the untouched break, top-right is busy jungle editing | CutProc11 / WarpCut | 1 |
| 2 | **Bounce** | Triplet feel (bounce chance), with halftime in the last zone | Ratchet climb (ratchet chance and climb together) | Ratchets get faster and rise in pitch together | Bounce | 1 |
| 3 | **Glitch** | Repeat rate: 1/16 to 1/128 (subdivision, repeats, stutter area) | Pitch step per repeat, from falling to rising | Faster repeats get crunchier: crusher bits fall as X rises | SQPusher + Crusher | 1 |
| 4 | **Suction** | Band centre, low to high | Squeeze: high-pass and low-pass close in while resonance rises | The filter breathes with each cut, and level is held so it never thins | Impact filter | 2 |
| 5 | **Filter** | Cutoff | Resonance and tail | Tuned tails ring at the ends of rolls, and the mix ducks to stay clean | Impact filter + Resonator | 2 |
| 6 | **Tape** | Stop length: half a beat to four beats | How often: phrase ends only, up to every beat | Stops land on the grid and spin back up into the next hit | TapeCut | 2 |
| 7 | **Loops** | Outer loop: one bar down to one beat | Depth: loops nested inside that loop, each shorter | Every level snaps to real hits | New engine work | 3 |
| 8 | **Rush** | Accelerate or drag into the bar line | Roll length | Rolls speed up into the downbeat or drag behind it | CutProc11 accel/ritard | 3 |

Grain is held in reserve. It can replace a program only if a prototype proves
it clean.

## Parameters and saved state

New parameters are appended with version hint 9 (`juce::ParameterID{…, 9}`),
and no existing identity, index or range changes. All are automatable unless
noted, so a performance can be recorded and played back exactly.

| ID | Type | Default | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `padProgram` | choice, 8 | Cut | Programs not yet built can't be selected |
| `padX`, `padY` | float 0–1 | 0.5 | |
| `padTouch` | bool | off | Z |
| `padHold` | bool | off | |
| `padQuantize` | choice: Beat, Bar | Beat | |
| `surface` | choice: Pad, Classic | Pad | Not automatable |

**Migration (our call).** A saved state without `surface` loads as
**Classic**: Livecut cuts continuously with its legacy settings, exactly as
0.1 and 0.2 did. This is explicit in `setStateInformation`, as an exception to
the rule that a missing parameter takes its default. New instances default to
Pad. The schema version becomes 9.

**Name clash to avoid.** Legacy "Audio Hold" (loop the last wet phrase) stays a
legacy control. The pad's Hold is a latch, and its internal ID is `padHold`.

## Engine changes (slice 1)

- **Restart cutting at the next unit boundary.** This ends the current phrase
  and plans a new one from the next unit boundary.
  - It keeps the render context, the raw history and the transport epoch,
    unlike `reset()` and `resetRendering()`.
  - No allocation, and safe for every buffer partition.
- **`PadGate`**, plain C++ in `src/engine`. It turns touch, Hold and Quantize
  into a sample-accurate wet gain:
  - engage is immediate (cutting itself starts on the grid);
  - release lands on the next beat or bar boundary of the render position,
    with a 5 ms fade;
  - a pending program switch fires on the same boundaries.
  - It runs per sample on the delayed render timeline, just before the
    cutter renders that sample, so its requests land on the same sample and
    timing is correct under any latency.
  - Its gain is applied to the wet signal before Audio Hold, the mixer and
    the Spectral stage.
- **The program mapping** (pure functions above) produces the Settings the
  stream renders with while `surface == Pad`.

## Editor (slice 1)

- **The pad page is the main page.**
- **The phrase ruler** reuses `ui::PhraseRuler`.
- **The pad** adapts `ui::XYPad` from the prototype branch
  (`codex/performance-prototype`):
  - It keeps host gestures, keyboard nudging and accessibility.
  - It adds `padTouch` begin/end on mouse down/up, and ends any open gesture
    when focus is lost or the pad is hidden.
- **The program buttons:** programs from later slices show as unavailable,
  not hidden, so the layout is final from slice 1.
- **The legacy pages** stay reachable from a small "Classic" switch until
  slice 4 decides their fate. Classic sessions open on them.

## Slices

1. **Foundation:** the surface, engage/release/Hold/Quantize, program
   switching, the parameters and migration, and Cut, Bounce and Glitch.
2. Suction, Filter and Tape.
3. Loops (nested looping, new engine work) and Rush.
4. **1.0 clean-up:** decide on the legacy pages, Studio and Spectral (the
   audit leans toward removal), refresh the docs and release 1.0.

Each slice is its own PR, green and playable.

## Quality gates (every slice)

- **RED/GREEN tests:**
  - mapping determinism and ranges;
  - engage and release timing, sample-accurate across buffer partitions;
  - restart on unit boundaries;
  - program switch on beat and bar boundaries;
  - Hold;
  - Classic migration that is sample-identical to before (existing legacy
    equivalence);
  - no allocation in the audio callback;
  - state round trip.
- pluginval strictness 10 and `auval` in CI.
- **By ear in Live (the owner):** each program across the whole pad, with no
  clicks on engage, release or switch, no mud, and musical everywhere.
  Verdicts go in the audit document.

## Known limits

- **Very short taps.** Parameters are read once per host buffer, so a press
  and release inside a single buffer (under about 10 ms at common sizes) isn't
  seen. Any longer tap cuts until the next beat.
- **Fade at cut edges.** Programs keep Fade at 0 ms, the setting the owner
  heard as click-free. Restarts are timed onto unit boundaries, where the
  classic cutter also changes cuts. If restarts or switches click in
  listening, the neutral base's fade becomes 2 ms **(our call, to be checked
  by ear)**.

## Left to the builder

The exact starting curves for each program, the visual details within the
