# Livecut 1.0: Rush

Date: 2026-11-02. This is the second program of pad slice 3, stacked on [Tape](2026-11-02-livecut-1.0-tape-design.md). Loops, the slice's other program, stays unavailable. The owner asked for the details to be decided here ("build what you think is best… canonical, modern, idiomatic"). Calls made here rather than by the owner are marked **(our call)**.

The parent spec is [the pad design](2026-11-01-livecut-1.0-pad-design.md). It describes Rush as:
- X: accelerate or drag into the bar line.
- Y: roll length.
- Rolls speed up into the downbeat or drag behind it.
- Engine: CutProc11 accel/ritard.

## What you hear

- **The track, then a roll into every bar line.** Rush plays the input as it is until a fixed point before each bar line. There it rolls the hit on that point into the next downbeat. The downbeat then arrives on time, and the track carries on.
- **Between rolls, the track, untouched.** Engaging Rush changes nothing you can hear until the first roll. Outside a roll's repeats the output is the input, sample for sample.
- **Y: how long the roll is.** One, two, three or four beats before the bar line, in four equal steps of Y **(our call)**. A roll never starts before its bar, so in 3/4 the top two steps are both the whole bar. At the top of Y in 4/4, the whole bar is a roll.
- **Every roll starts on a hit.** A roll starts a whole number of beats before the bar line, so it repeats what the beat there holds. In 4/4 at the four steps that's beat 4, 3, 2 or 1: usually a snare or a kick.
- **X: drag or rush.**
  - **Centre:** an even roll of 16ths.
  - **Right:** the repeats speed up into the bar line, rising in pitch.
  - **Left:** they slow down into the bar line, falling in pitch.
  - The curve grows stronger toward each edge. At the right edge the roll runs from about a dotted 8th down to a 64th, eight times faster, and rises a fourth. The left edge is its mirror.
- **Every roll swells into the downbeat.** The hit plays at full level. Its repeats then start 12 dB down and swell back to full level by the bar line, so the downbeat lands **(our call)**.
- **Cuts never become a buzz.** No roll cut is shorter than 1/32 of a beat. In practice the shortest is about 1/15 of a beat (a 64th note): 33 ms at 120 bpm.
- **Straight** keeps every cut on even divisions of the beat. The curve becomes steps, each twice as fast as the last.
- **Deterministic.** Rush makes no random choices. The same spot in the same bar always plays the same roll.
- **Switching away lets the roll go.** A switch in the middle of a roll fades the repeat out under the next program over 5 ms, rather than cutting it off **(our call)**.

## Why a new procedure, and not CutProc11 (our call)

The survey found that CutProc11 has no accelerando or ritardando. Its stutter rolls are even, start wherever a regular block happens to end, and use the phrase's random draws. Rush needs three things CutProc11 can't give without changing its draws:
- a roll of an exact length, ending exactly on the bar line;
- a curve across the roll;
- no random choices at all.

So Rush is its own procedure, `Procedure::rush`, and CutProc11's draws stay untouched. The parent's "CutProc11 accel/ritard" is honoured in spirit: the roll is the same mechanism as CutProc11's stutter (one block whose cuts replay the block's first frame), with an exact length and a curve.

## Engine

### The procedure

`Procedure` gains `rush`, appended after `bounce`. The `procedure` parameter keeps its four choices, so Classic can never select it, and its automation doesn't change. `Cutter::procedureFor` returns a new `Rush` (in `CutProcedures.h`, `Rush.cpp`).

`CutParameters` gains three fields, used only by Rush:
- `double rollBeats = 0`: the roll's length before each bar line, in beats. 0 plays no roll.
- `double rollCurve = 0`: in [−1, 1]. −1 drags hardest, 0 is even, +1 rushes hardest.
- `double rollCents = 0`: the pitch of the roll's last cut, relative to the first.

The defaults play no roll. So if the parameters change to Classic's mid-bar (a switch to the Classic surface), Rush plays the track to its bar line, and Classic takes over there.

**Phrases are one bar** (`choosePhraseBars` returns 1 without a draw), so every bar line ends a roll.

**Blocks.** With `B` beats in the bar, `β = unitsPerBar / B` units per beat, and the block starting `s` beats into the bar:
- `T = min (rollBeats, B)`, and the roll starts at `r = B − T`.
- If `T` is 0, or `round (4T)` is under 2 (a bar shorter than 3/8 of a beat): one track cut to the bar line.
- If `s < r`: one track cut to `r`.
- If `s = r`: the roll, `T` beats long.
- If `s > r` (the pad engaged after the roll should have started): the roll starts at the next whole number of beats before the bar line, `r' = B − ⌊B − s⌋`.
  - If `r' > s`: one track cut to `r'`, then the roll of `B − r'` beats.
  - If `r' = B`: one track cut to the bar line. No roll this bar.
  - So a late engage gets a shorter roll that still starts on a beat and lands on the bar line, never a squashed one **(our call)**.
- Comparisons use a tolerance of 10⁻⁹ beats, so a block that ends an ulp off its point counts as on it.
- A track cut is unpitched, at unity, with gate 1. It's the first cut of its block, so it plays the input live.

**The roll (Straight off).** `n = round (4T)` cuts, so an even roll is 16ths **(our call)**.
- `S = 8^c`, with `c = rollCurve`: the ratio of the first cut's length to the last's.
- The lengths form a geometric series from first to last, as in WarpCut. With `a = c · ln 8 / (n − 1)`:
  - `u₀ = T · (1 − e^−a) / (1 − e^−na)`, computed with `expm1` so it stays exact near the centre;
  - `u_k = u₀ · e^−ka`;
  - `u_k = T / n` when `c = 0`.
- **The last cut takes the exact remainder** of the bar, in units, so the roll lands exactly on the bar line.
- **The floor holds by construction.** With `S ≤ 8` and `n = round (4T) ≥ 2` (so `T ≥ 3/8`), the shortest cut is at least `T / 9` at `n = 2`, and about `ln 8 / 28 ≈ 0.074` beat for long rolls. So it's never under 1/24 of a beat, a third above the floor of 1/32; `Rush.cpp` asserts it. At `T = 1` the shortest is 1/15 of a beat, at `T = 4` 0.073. The first plan's loop that dropped `n` under the floor could never fire, so the build doesn't have it.
- **Why geometric, not the survey's power curve (our call).**
  - A power curve `t_k = T (k/n)^p` changes speed fast at first, then flattens out, so its rush stops rushing before the bar line.
  - A geometric series changes speed by the same ratio at every cut. That is how the ear hears acceleration (in octaves of rate), and it is what Straight's halvings quantise.

**The roll (Straight on).** The curve becomes steps.
- `m = round (3 |c|)` halvings, so the stepped ratio `2^m` is the continuous `8^|c|` rounded to the nearest octave.
- `m = 0`: cuts of ¼ beat.
- `m ≥ 1`: each step `j < m` plays cuts of `½ · 2^−j` beats until the roll is `1 − 2^−(j+1)` of the way through. The rest is cuts of `½ · 2^−m`. For example:
  - a one-beat rush with `m = 3` is ½, ¼, ⅛, 1/16, 1/16;
  - a four-beat rush is four 8ths, four 16ths, four 32nds and eight 64ths.
- A drag plays the same lengths in reverse.
- The last cut takes the remainder. Every length is a whole number of 64ths of a beat, and so is every boundary.
- The shortest cut is 1/16 of a beat. Only a bar off the 64th grid (a meter like 7/32) leaves a remainder shorter than the floor; it joins the cut before it.

**Each roll cut** (index `k` of `n`):
- gate 1, centred;
- **pitch** `rollCents · k / (n − 1)`. Cut 0, the hit itself, keeps its pitch;
- **level** 0 dB for cut 0. Its repeats swell from −12 dB to 0 dB, even in decibels: `−12 (1 − (k − 1) / (n − 2))` dB for `n ≥ 3`, and 0 dB for a single repeat.

**Pitch and the recording.** A replay pitched up reads its source faster than it plays. Cut `k` replays `u_k` of the source from the roll's start, `t_k` after it. So it stays behind the recording while `u_k (rate − 1) < t_k`. In a rush, `u_k ≤ u₀ = t₁ ≤ t_k`, and the rate is at most `2^(5/12)`, so every rush cut plays to its end. A drag is pitched down and never catches the recording.

### Why the swell (our call)

A roll repeats the start of a hit, its loudest part, many times. Measured on the review's drum loop at 120 bpm, over eight bars, with even rolls at unity:
- a full-bar roll of the downbeat's kick lifts drums alone by **5.8 dB**;
- a two-beat roll of beat 3's kick lifts it by 4.2 dB, and the roll itself by 7.6 dB;
- Cut, at its busiest, lifts the same drums by 1.3 to 1.6 dB.

That would be a level jump. A level hold measured from the audio can't be causal here: the audio a roll replaces hasn't arrived when the roll starts. And holding a roll to the groove's power would turn its hits down to a weak patter.

A swell is what a drummer plays: a roll that builds into the downbeat. Its last repeats are at full level, so its peaks are the hit's own, and it lands the downbeat. Measured with the prototype (`LivePlayer` driven with Rush's layouts) over eight bars:

| Repeats start at | Drums alone | Drums + 55 Hz sub | Drums + 150 Hz bass |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 dB (no swell) | −1.5 to **+5.8** dB | −0.7 to +1.7 dB | −0.3 to +2.3 dB |
| −6 dB | −2.5 to +3.8 dB | −2.3 to +0.6 dB | −1.9 to +0.5 dB |
| **−12 dB** | −3.1 to **+2.4** dB | −3.3 to **+0.05** dB | −2.8 to −0.1 dB |
| −18 dB | −3.4 to +1.6 dB | −4.4 to −0.3 dB | −4.0 to −0.3 dB |

At −12 dB, Rush is never louder than the input with a bass line under the drums. Drums alone with a kick rolled for a whole bar are the worst case. −18 dB would hollow out rolls over a bass line, so −12 dB is the balance. The by-ear check decides.

Through the real Cutter (eight bars, 20 spots: X at 0, ¼, ½, ¾ and 1, rolls of one to four beats), the build measures:

| Material | Rush against the input | Cut's busiest spot against the input |
|---|---|---|
| Drums alone | −3.3 to **+2.4** dB | +1.6 dB |
| Drums + 55 Hz sub | −3.3 to +0.05 dB | +0.8 dB |
| Drums + 150 Hz bass | −2.8 to −0.1 dB | +0.5 dB |

Rush never peaks above the input: a repeat replays the input at unity or below, and a pitched one interpolates between two of its samples.

### A cut with no fade

`Cut` gains `bool fade = true`, in its padding, so `Cut` doesn't grow. With `fade` false, `LivePlayer` plays the cut with no fade envelope.
- The pad's base fade is 0 ms, which the player renders as a one-frame fade: every cut's first sample is 0.
- That is harmless at a splice. But where a cut continues the audio before it, it's a one-sample dropout. The Tape review measured such dropouts as full-scale clicks on sustained material.
- In Rush, three joins continue the audio:
  - engaging (the input, then the first track cut);
  - the track into the roll's first cut, the hit itself;
  - a run-on frame (below) into the next block.
- So every Rush cut has `fade` false, and the track is bitwise the input.
- At a roll's splices the one-frame fade made no difference: it swaps the step for two half-steps around a zero.
- No other procedure sets `fade` false, so Classic and every other program are bit-identical.

### Every frame until the next block

As Tape found, the player lays a block out in samples from its first frame, while the next block starts on the musical grid. When a bar isn't a whole number of samples, which is true at most tempos, the two can be a frame apart. A block that ends a frame early used to render a frame of silence.
- **For a Rush block, a frame after its last cut plays the input** (the raw frame from the history). After a track cut that's the track itself. After a roll it's the next bar's track arriving a frame early, at the splice the roll ends on anyway.
- **A block that runs a frame long** is cut short by the next one, as before.
- **Through tempo automation** a Rush block counts its elapsed time exactly, as Tape's do (`LivePlayer::setExactRetime`). So its run-ons stay short, and a roll still ends on its bar line (measured below).
- Classic and the other programs keep their behaviour, so their bits don't change.

The Cutter's block plan records whether it is a Rush block. Activation sets the exact count, and `renderSource` plays the run-on.

- Measured at every whole-number tempo from 80 to 180 bpm at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz, and in 3/4, 5/4, 7/4, 5/8 and 13/8 at 93, 120 and 140 bpm, a roll's last cut ends within a frame of the bar line, either side.
- Through ramps of ±0.5 and ±3 bpm a beat, with 64- and 512-frame buffers, each re-time rounds its cut's start. So up to 2 frames of input can come before the bar line, or the last cut can lose up to 4 frames (under 0.1 ms) to it.

### Switching away (our call)

A restart that cuts off a Rush cut, such as a program switch, lets the cut play on as a tail under what follows, as Tape's motions do.
- **The crossfade.** Over 5 ms, the output fades linearly from the tail to the new program. It starts wholly on the tail, which continues the sample before the switch.
- **The tail** is `LivePlayer::Tail`: plain data taken from the player at the restart (the block's source, the cut's read position and rate, and its stereo matrix). It reads the history as the cut would have, with no fade, as Rush's cuts have none.
- **Why.** Spliced, a switch in the middle of a roll put the repeat straight against the next program's first cut. On the processor's test sine, Rush → Tape stepped 1.33: 4.2 times the larger of the two programs played throughout in the same window. Faded, it steps 1.0 times.
- **On the track** the tail is the track itself, so the crossfade changes nothing a program that starts on the track would play.
- **Only a Rush plan takes one,** so no other program's switch changes. A jump in the host's position (a seek, a loop that wraps) resets the rendering as for every program, with no tail.

## Pad

- `isAvailable (rush)` is true. Captions: X "Push", Y "Roll" **(our call)**.
- **Mapping, with x and y in [0, 1]:**
  - procedure `rush`, 16 units per bar (so engaging lands on the next 16th), seed 808 (unused: Rush draws nothing);
  - `rollCurve = 2x − 1`;
  - `rollCents = 500 (2x − 1)`, a fourth up at the right and down at the left **(our call)**;
  - `rollBeats = {1, 2, 3, 4}[min (⌊4y⌋, 3)]`, four equal steps with no hysteresis;
  - the base's other `CutParameters`. Straight comes from the pad's switch, as for every program.
- **There are no discrete zones,** so moving the pad never restarts the cutting. Changes take effect from the next block: the roll's start, or the bar line.
- The pad page gives Rush a tooltip in the voice of the others, and the README gets one sentence.
- The curves are starting points, tuned by ear. The verdict goes in the listening audit.

- **Pinned first.** Before any engine change:

  - extend the 97 bpm event hash (a restart, an idle toggle and two tempo changes, a bar that isn't whole samples) with Tape, and with Classic's CutProc11, WarpCut, SQPusher (Straight) and Bounce at fades of 0 and 2 ms, pitched and panned, measured on the unchanged engine.

  These, `LegacyEquivalenceTests` and every existing hash stay unchanged. Rush joins both hashes as built, so the next slice can show it unchanged.
- **The review's fuzz.** Base (this branch's parent) against head, bitwise, over 400 random scenarios: 8, 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz, meters 4, 3, 3.5, 2.5 and 6, every Classic procedure and Fracture family, Cut, Bounce, Glitch, Suction, Filter and Tape, with restarts, idle, tempo changes, seeks, `resetRendering` and random partitions. - **Rush's own fuzz.** 60 random scenarios at 8, 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz, in eight meters, with random spots, Straight, restarts, idle, tempo steps and seeks: one-frame, random and 1,024-frame partitions are bitwise identical, with no NaN, no cutter error, and a peak never above the input's. 30 of them are also clean with address and undefined-behaviour sanitizers and assertions on.
- **The layout (pure), over x and y, meters 4, 3, 5, 7, 2.5, 3.5, 6.5, 1, 0.5 and 0.25, and every unit to engage on:**
  - Every block keeps the procedure contract. Rush draws nothing from the random generator.
  - The roll ends exactly on the bar line: the cuts sum to the bar within 10⁻⁹ units.
  - Engaged from the bar line, the roll starts `min (rollBeats, B)` before the bar line, has `round (4T)` cuts, and the bar's other block is one track cut.
  - The first-to-last ratio is `8^c` within 10⁻⁹, with every cut shorter than the one before in a rush, longer in a drag, and equal at the centre.
  - No roll cut is shorter than 1/32 beat. Over every meter from ⅛ to 8 beats in eighths, the shortest is 1/24 of a beat (3/8 of a beat in two cuts, 8:1).
  - Engaged late, the roll starts on the next whole beat before the bar line, never before the engage.
  - Pitch and level: cut 0 at 0 cents and unity, the last cut at `rollCents` and unity, the repeats even in decibels from −12 dB.
  - `rollBeats` 0 gives one track cut to the bar line.
  - With Straight, every cut is a whole number of 64ths of a beat, and the step ratio is `2^m`.
- **Through the Cutter:**
  - **The track, bitwise.** At every whole-number tempo from 80 to 180 bpm, at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz, Rush is the input outside its rolls' repeats, sample for sample.
  - **Rolls land.** At those tempos and in 3/4, 5/4, 7/4, 5/8 and 13/8, each new bar starts within half a sample of the host's bar line, each roll plays all its cuts, and its last cut ends within a frame of the bar line. Through tempo ramps of ±0.5 and ±3 bpm a beat, with 64- and 512-frame buffers, within 2 frames of input before it or 4 frames of the cut after it (measured 2 and 4).
  - No frame drops to silence.
  - **Arrangement loops.** A two-bar loop that wraps on its bar line, in 4/4 and 3/4 at 93 and 120 bpm, lands each roll on the loop's end and opens the next pass on the track, exactly.
  - **A restart mid-roll** crossfades from the repeat to what follows over 5 ms, within 10⁻⁶ of the weighted sum of the two. It steps no more than the repeat or the input do around it.
  - **Pitched repeats play to their end.** Every roll cut's playable samples equal its sounding samples.
  - **On drums** (the review's loop, alone, with a 55 Hz sub and with a 150 Hz bass), at 5 × 4 spots:
    - the peak never exceeds the input's;
    - with a bass line, the RMS is never more than 0.25 dB over the input's (measured +0.05 dB);
    - on drums alone, never more than 2.5 dB over the input's (measured +2.4 dB), and never more than 1 dB over Cut's busiest spot on the same loop (measured +0.84 dB);
    - never more than 3.5 dB under the input at any spot (measured −3.3 dB).
    - Without the swell, the drums alone rise 5.8 dB and the bars fail.
- **Processor:**
  - **Engage is seamless.** From the touch until the first roll, the output equals the input, bitwise.
  - **Engage, release and switch** on Rush leave the cutter error-free. Release returns exactly to the input.
  - **Switch clicks.** Within ±2400 samples of the switch, against the larger of the two programs played throughout in the same window:
    - Rush → Cut in the middle of a whole-bar roll: at most 0.5× (measured 0.27×);
    - Rush → Tape there: at most 1.25× (measured 1.0×; spliced, it was 4.2×);
    - Cut → Rush: at most 2× (measured 1.7×). This is Cut's last cut meeting Rush's track, the splice any restart out of Cut makes;
    - Rush → Classic, around the bar line where Classic's procedure takes over, before the roll and during it: at most 1× (measured 0.36× and 0.75×).
  - **An arrangement loop** through the processor's epoch path opens on the track.
  - **Determinism.** Engaging at the same spot in the same bar after a different history gives bit-identical output.
  - **Every buffer partition** gives identical output, at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz.
  - **At 93, 124 and 140 bpm**, at each rate, engaged Rush is the input bitwise until its first roll, and its rolls land on the bar line.
  - **No allocation** in the callback.
- **The Rush mapping:** ranges, determinism, the formulas above, no restarts while moving, and `padSettings` carrying them.
- pluginval strictness 10 and auval in CI.
- **By ear in Live (owner):** the whole pad, with:
  - no clicks on engage, release or switch;
  - rolls that lead into the downbeat and land it;
  - no buzz at the fast end;
  - no mud from rolled kicks or bass.

## Known limits

- **A roll on sustained material is a run of splices.** Each repeat restarts the hit. On a held bass note each restart is a step, as in every stutter roll in Cut. On drums the restart is the hit's own attack. Measured over eight bars at 20 spots, Rush's largest step on a held 55 Hz bass is 0.90, against Cut's 0.78 at its 9 spots; on drums with a sub, 1.42 against Cut's 1.47.
- **A rolled kick with a sub rolls the sub too.** The repeats restart the low end, a thump each. The swell keeps the early ones quiet.
- **The swell's first repeat drops 12 dB below the hit.** Over a dense mix that is a short hollow before the build. It's the by-ear risk most worth checking.
- **The source is a beat, not a detected hit.** In 4/4 the beats are where kicks and snares sit, but a roll in music with nothing on the beat repeats whatever is there.
- **Late engages.** Engaging after the roll should have started gives a shorter roll from the next beat, or none if the bar's last beat has begun. From the next bar on, every roll is the full length.
- **Pitch is resampling,** with linear interpolation, as the Classic pages pitch: at most a fourth either way.
- **At very fast tempos** the floor in beats is short in time: the shortest cut, 1/15 of a beat, falls under 10 ms above 400 bpm.
- **Classic and the other programs keep a one-frame fade and their dropped frame** at a block that ends early. Changing that would change their bits.
- **A switch into Rush is a splice** from the last program's cut onto Rush's track, as any restart out of that program is: 1.7× from Cut. A switch out of Rush fades.
- **A jump in the host's position cuts a roll where it is.** A seek, or an arrangement loop whose end isn't a bar line, resets the rendering mid-roll with no tail, as for every program; the host's own audio jumps there too. A loop that ends on a bar line meets the roll's own landing.
- **Through tempo ramps a roll lands within 0.1 ms,** not within a frame: see the run-on above.
- **At 44.1 kHz, a restart on an odd 16th can land a sample apart, depending on the host's buffer size.** This predates Rush, as Filter's spec records. Rush's own blocks start on whole beats and bar lines.
