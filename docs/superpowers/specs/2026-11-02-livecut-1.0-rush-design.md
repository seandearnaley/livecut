# Livecut 1.0: Rush

Date: 2026-11-02. This is the second program of pad slice 3, stacked on [Tape](2026-11-02-livecut-1.0-tape-design.md). Loops, the slice's other program, stays unavailable. The owner asked for the details to be decided here ("build what you think is best… canonical, modern, idiomatic"). Calls made here rather than by the owner are marked **(our call)**.

The parent spec is [the pad design](2026-11-01-livecut-1.0-pad-design.md). It describes Rush as:
- X: accelerate or drag into the bar line.
- Y: roll length.
- Rolls speed up into the downbeat or drag behind it.
- Engine: CutProc11 accel/ritard.

## What you hear

- **The track, then a roll into every bar line.** Rush plays the input as it is until a fixed point before each bar line. There it rolls the hit on that point into the next downbeat. The downbeat then arrives on time, and the track carries on.
- **Between rolls, the track, untouched.** Engaging Rush changes nothing you can hear until the first roll. Outside a roll's repeats, and the 2 ms where a roll hands back to the track, the output is the input, sample for sample.
- **Y: how long the roll is.** One, two, three or four beats before the bar line, in four equal steps of Y **(our call)**. A roll never starts before its bar, so in 3/4 the top two steps are both the whole bar. At the top of Y in 4/4, the whole bar is a roll.
- **Every roll starts on a beat.** A roll starts a whole number of beats before the bar line, so it repeats what the beat there holds. In 4/4 at the four steps that's beat 4, 3, 2 or 1: usually a snare or a kick. In a meter of eighths (5/8, 7/8, 13/8) the bar ends half a beat after its last whole beat, so a roll starts on an off-beat eighth: in 5/8, one and a half beats or half a beat into the bar.
- **X: drag or rush.**
  - **Centre:** an even roll of 16ths.
  - **Right:** the repeats speed up into the bar line, rising in pitch.
  - **Left:** they slow down into the bar line, falling in pitch.
  - The curve grows stronger toward each edge. At the right edge the roll runs from about a dotted 8th down to a 64th, eight times faster, and rises a fourth. The left edge is its mirror.
- **Every roll swells into the downbeat.** The hit plays at full level. Its repeats then start 12 dB down and swell back to full level by the bar line, so the downbeat lands **(our call)**.
- **Cuts never become a buzz.** No roll cut is shorter than 30 ms, nor 1/32 of a beat **(our call)**. Up to 120 bpm the floor never bites: the shortest cuts are about 1/15 of a beat for a continuous rush (33 ms at 120 bpm) and a 64th note, 1/16 of a beat, at the end of Straight's rush (31.25 ms).
- **Straight** keeps every cut on even divisions of the beat. The curve becomes steps, each twice as fast as the last.
- **Deterministic.** Rush makes no random choices. The same spot in the same bar always plays the same roll.
- **Joins don't click.** Each repeat, and the bar line where a roll hands back to the track, crossfades over 2 ms, so a held bass or a pad chord never clicks there. The downbeat after a roll fades in over its first 2 ms, and each repeat's attack ramps in **(owner's ruling)**.
- **Switches fade both ways.** A switch out of Rush, wherever it lands (in a cut, on a beat or on the bar line), fades what Rush was playing out under the next program over 5 ms. A switch into Rush fades the last program's cut out under Rush's track the same way **(our call)**.

## Why a new procedure, and not CutProc11 (our call)

The survey found that CutProc11 has no accelerando or ritardando. Its stutter rolls are even, start wherever a regular block happens to end, and use the phrase's random draws. Rush needs three things CutProc11 can't give without changing its draws:
- a roll of an exact length, ending exactly on the bar line;
- a curve across the roll;
- no random choices at all.

So Rush is its own procedure, `Procedure::rush`, and CutProc11's draws stay untouched. The parent's "CutProc11 accel/ritard" is honoured in spirit: the roll is the same mechanism as CutProc11's stutter (one block whose cuts replay the block's first frame), with an exact length and a curve.

## Engine

### The procedure

`Procedure` gains `rush`, appended after `bounce`. The `procedure` parameter keeps its four choices, so Classic can never select it, and its automation doesn't change. `Cutter::procedureFor` returns a new `Rush` (in `CutProcedures.h`, `Rush.cpp`).

`CutParameters` gains three fields, used only by Rush:
- `double rollBeats = 0`: the roll's length before each bar line, in beats. 0 plays no roll.
- `double rollCurve = 0`: in [−1, 1]. −1 drags hardest, 0 is even, +1 rushes hardest.
- `double rollCents = 0`: the pitch of the roll's last cut, relative to the first.

The defaults play no roll. So if the parameters change to Classic's mid-bar (a switch to the Classic surface), Rush plays the track to its bar line, and Classic takes over there.

**Phrases are one bar** (`choosePhraseBars` returns 1 without a draw), so every bar line ends a roll.

**Blocks.** With `B` beats in the bar, `β = unitsPerBar / B` units per beat, and the block starting `s` beats into the bar:
- `T = min (rollBeats, B)`, and the roll starts at `r = B − T`.
- If `T` is 0, or `round (4T)` is under 2 (a bar shorter than 3/8 of a beat): one track cut to the bar line.
- If `s < r`: one track cut to `r`.
- If `s = r`: the roll, `T` beats long.
- If `s > r` (the pad engaged after the roll should have started): the roll starts at the next whole number of beats before the bar line, `r' = B − ⌊B − s⌋`.
  - If `r' > s`: one track cut to `r'`, then the roll of `B − r'` beats.
  - If `r' = B`: one track cut to the bar line. No roll this bar.
  - So a late engage gets a shorter roll that still starts on a beat and lands on the bar line, never a squashed one **(our call)**.
- Comparisons use a tolerance of 10⁻⁹ beats, so a block that ends an ulp off its point counts as on it.
- A track cut is unpitched, at unity, with gate 1. It's the first cut of its block, so it plays the input live.

**The roll (Straight off).** `n = round (4T)` cuts, so an even roll is 16ths **(our call)**.
- `S = 8^c`, with `c = rollCurve`: the ratio of the first cut's length to the last's.
- The lengths form a geometric series from first to last, as in WarpCut. With `a = c · ln 8 / (n − 1)`:
  - `u₀ = T · (1 − e^−a) / (1 − e^−na)`, computed with `expm1` so it stays exact near the centre;
  - `u_k = u₀ · e^−ka`;
  - `u_k = T / n` when `c = 0`.
- **The last cut takes the exact remainder** of the bar, in units, so the roll lands exactly on the bar line.
- **The floor of 1/32 beat holds by construction.** With `S ≤ 8` and `n = round (4T) ≥ 2` (so `T ≥ 3/8`), the shortest cut is at least `T / 9` at `n = 2`, and about `ln 8 / 28 ≈ 0.074` beat for long rolls. So it's never under 1/24 of a beat; `Rush.cpp` asserts it. At `T = 1` the shortest is 1/15 of a beat, at `T = 4` 0.073.
- **The floor of 30 ms (our call)** comes from the tempo, which the cutter passes in `BlockContext::beatsPerSecond` (the other procedures ignore it).
  - **Why 30 ms.** Under about 30 ms (over 33 Hz) a run of repeats starts to fuse into a pitched buzz. At 120 bpm every roll's shortest cut is already longer, so the floor changes nothing up to 120 bpm. Without it, Straight's whole-bar rush ended in eight 64ths of 20.8 ms (a 48 Hz repetition) at 180 bpm, and the continuous rush's last cuts were 22 to 24 ms.
  - Where it bites, a continuous roll first takes fewer cuts, `n ≤ ⌊T / floor⌋`, then the largest curve that keeps its shortest cut on the floor, found by bisection. Its direction is kept.
  - With no tempo (the procedure tested alone), there is no time floor.
- **Why geometric, not the survey's power curve (our call).**
  - A power curve `t_k = T (k/n)^p` changes speed fast at first, then flattens out, so its rush stops rushing before the bar line.
  - A geometric series changes speed by the same ratio at every cut. That is how the ear hears acceleration (in octaves of rate), and it is what Straight's halvings quantise.

**The roll (Straight on).** The curve becomes steps.
- `m = round (3 |c|)` halvings, so the stepped ratio `2^m` is the continuous `8^|c|` rounded to the nearest octave.
- `m = 0`: cuts of ¼ beat.
- `m ≥ 1`: each step `j < m` plays cuts of `½ · 2^−j` beats until the roll is `1 − 2^−(j+1)` of the way through. The rest is cuts of `½ · 2^−m`. For example:
  - a one-beat rush with `m = 3` is ½, ¼, ⅛, 1/16, 1/16;
  - a four-beat rush is four 8ths, four 16ths, four 32nds and eight 64ths.
- A drag plays the same lengths in reverse.
- The last cut takes the remainder. Every length is a whole number of 64ths of a beat, and so is every boundary.
- The shortest cut is 1/16 of a beat. The 30 ms floor takes halvings away (`m` drops while `½ · 2^−m` is under it), then doubles the even roll's quarter-beat cuts.
- Only a bar off the 64th grid (a meter like 7/32), or a doubled even roll, leaves a remainder shorter than the floor; it joins the cut before it.
- A roll the floor leaves with no room for a repeat plays the track to the bar line.

**Each roll cut** (index `k` of `n`):
- gate 1, centred;
- **pitch** `rollCents · k / (n − 1)`. Cut 0, the hit itself, keeps its pitch;
- **level** 0 dB for cut 0. Its repeats swell from −12 dB to 0 dB, even in decibels: `−12 (1 − (k − 1) / (n − 2))` dB for `n ≥ 3`, and 0 dB for a single repeat.

**Pitch and the recording.** A replay pitched up reads its source faster than it plays. Cut `k` replays `u_k` of the source from the roll's start, `t_k` after it. So it stays behind the recording while `u_k (rate − 1) < t_k`. In a rush, `u_k ≤ u₀ = t₁ ≤ t_k`, and the rate is at most `2^(5/12)`, so every rush cut plays to its end. A drag is pitched down and never catches the recording.

### Why the swell (our call)

A roll repeats the start of a hit, its loudest part, many times. Measured on the review's drum loop at 120 bpm, over eight bars, with even rolls at unity:
- a full-bar roll of the downbeat's kick lifts drums alone by **5.8 dB**;
- a two-beat roll of beat 3's kick lifts it by 4.2 dB, and the roll itself by 7.6 dB;
- Cut, at its busiest, lifts the same drums by 1.3 to 1.6 dB.

That would be a level jump. A level hold measured from the audio can't be causal here: the audio a roll replaces hasn't arrived when the roll starts. And holding a roll to the groove's power would turn its hits down to a weak patter.

A swell is what a drummer plays: a roll that builds into the downbeat. Its last repeats are at full level, so its peaks are the hit's own, and it lands the downbeat. Measured with the prototype (`LivePlayer` driven with Rush's layouts) over eight bars:

| Repeats start at | Drums alone | Drums + 55 Hz sub | Drums + 150 Hz bass |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 dB (no swell) | −1.5 to **+5.8** dB | −0.7 to +1.7 dB | −0.3 to +2.3 dB |
| −6 dB | −2.5 to +3.8 dB | −2.3 to +0.6 dB | −1.9 to +0.5 dB |
| **−12 dB** | −3.1 to **+2.4** dB | −3.3 to **+0.05** dB | −2.8 to −0.1 dB |
| −18 dB | −3.4 to +1.6 dB | −4.4 to −0.3 dB | −4.0 to −0.3 dB |

At −12 dB, Rush is never louder than the input with a bass line under the drums. Drums alone with a kick rolled for a whole bar are the worst case. −18 dB would hollow out rolls over a bass line, so −12 dB is the balance. The by-ear check decides.

Through the real Cutter (eight bars, 20 spots: X at 0, ¼, ½, ¾ and 1, rolls of one to four beats), the build measures:

| Material | Rush against the input | Cut's busiest spot against the input |
|---|---|---|
| Drums alone | −3.4 to **+2.35** dB | +1.6 dB |
| Drums + 55 Hz sub | −3.3 to +0.05 dB | +0.8 dB |
| Drums + 150 Hz bass | −2.9 to −0.1 dB | +0.5 dB |

These are with the joins crossfaded; before, each was within 0.08 dB of the same.

Rush never peaks above the input: a repeat replays the input at unity or below, and a pitched one interpolates between two of its samples.

### A cut with no fade

`Cut` gains `bool fade = true`, in its padding, so `Cut` doesn't grow. With `fade` false, `LivePlayer` plays the cut with no fade envelope.
- The pad's base fade is 0 ms, which the player renders as a one-frame fade: every cut's first sample is 0.
- That is harmless at a splice. But where a cut continues the audio before it, it's a one-sample dropout. The Tape review measured such dropouts as full-scale clicks on sustained material.
- In Rush, three joins continue the audio:
  - engaging (the input, then the first track cut);
  - the track into the roll's first cut, the hit itself;
  - a run-on frame (below) into the next block.
- So every Rush cut has `fade` false, and the track is bitwise the input.
- A roll's own joins, where the one-frame fade made no difference, crossfade instead (below).
- No other procedure sets `fade` false, so Classic and every other program are bit-identical.

### Every frame until the next block

As Tape found, the player lays a block out in samples from its first frame, while the next block starts on the musical grid. When a bar isn't a whole number of samples, which is true at most tempos, the two can be a frame apart. A block that ends a frame early used to render a frame of silence.
- **For a Rush block, a frame after its last cut plays the input** (the raw frame from the history). After a track cut that's the track itself. After a roll it's the next bar's track arriving a frame early, at the splice the roll ends on anyway.
- **A block that runs a frame long** is cut short by the next one, as before.
- **Through tempo automation** a Rush block counts its elapsed time exactly, as Tape's do (`LivePlayer::setExactRetime`). So its run-ons stay short, and a roll still ends on its bar line (measured below).
- Classic and the other programs keep their behaviour, so their bits don't change.

The Cutter's block plan records whether it is a Rush block. Activation sets the exact count, and `renderSource` plays the run-on.

- Measured at every whole-number tempo from 80 to 180 bpm at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz, and in 3/4, 5/4, 7/4, 5/8 and 13/8 at 93, 120 and 140 bpm, a roll's last cut ends within a frame of the bar line, either side.
- Through ramps of ±0.5 and ±3 bpm a beat, with the tempo changing every 1, 64, 512 or 2048 frames, each re-time rounds its cut's start. So up to 4 frames of input can come before the bar line, or the last cut can lose up to 4 frames (under 0.1 ms) to it.

### Half-sample ties

At 44.1 kHz many whole-number tempos put a bar line, or a whole beat where a roll starts, exactly half a sample between two frames: 128 bpm in 4/4, 96 and 160 in 3/4 and 5/4, 96 in 7/8, and 80, 96, 112, 128, 144 and 160 in 5/8 and 13/8 among others.
- **What went wrong.** The first build planned a block on the frame the cutter's bar wrap took, but the activation compare could take the next frame. The block's first cut then played the input a sample late for a whole block, and the join after it skipped a sample. Which frame won depended on rounding in the position accumulated since the host buffer began, so the output depended on the buffer size. The review counted 15.2 M track frames off the input in its tie sweep, and 22 of 1,818 configurations that depended on the partition.
- **The fix, for Rush plans only.**
  - A Rush block reads from the frame it activates on, so its first cut is the input.
  - A Rush plan counts a boundary within 10⁻⁹ beats as reached, in the bar wrap, the phrase end, the block end and the activation. A tie then always goes to the earlier frame, whatever the buffer.
- **Measured.** The review's tie sweep shows 0 track frames off the input, and its partition sweep 0 of 1,818 configurations.

### A roll's joins (owner's ruling)

Each repeat restarts the hit, and the bar line hands a roll back to the track. On sustained material each was a splice: on a held 0.6 bass the review counted clicks at 259 of 280 bar lines and 8.8 repeats a bar, with steps up to 0.60 at 120 bpm and 1.20 at 133. Clicks are a hard anti-goal, so the owner ruled for a crossfade, accepting that the downbeat isn't the input bitwise for 2 ms.
- **The crossfade.** At each join the cut before runs on past its end, as `LivePlayer::Tail`, and the output fades linearly from it to what follows over 2 ms. It starts wholly on the tail, which continues the sample before.
- **Where.** At every repeat; at the bar line after a roll, or at its run-on frame if it ends a frame early; and at a jump in the host's position (a seek or a loop's wrap), if rendering carries on from the next frame. A join that would begin after a pause in rendering is dropped.
- **Not at the roll's start.** The track runs straight into the hit, the same audio, so that join stays sample for sample.
- **Measured** with the review's harness, 8 bars at 20 spots, Straight on and off:

| Join, 48 kHz | Held 55 Hz bass, 120 bpm | Held bass, 133 bpm | Pad chord, 120 / 133 bpm | Drums, 120 bpm |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Repeats, before | 8.8 clicks a bar, max 0.60 | 8.2 a bar, max 1.13 | 8.2 / 8.3 a bar, max 0.96 / 1.02 | 3.5 a bar, max 0.75 |
| Repeats, after | **0**, max 0.005 | **0**, max 0.005 | **0**, max 0.029 / 0.025 | 0.3 a bar, max 0.39 |
| Bar line, before | 259 of 280, max 0.58 | 177 of 280, max 1.20 | 269 / 187 of 280, max 0.94 / 0.88 | 0, max 1.6× the local input |
| Bar line, after | **0**, max 0.005 | **0**, max 0.014 | **0**, max 0.028 / 0.030 | 0, max 0.37 |

  A click is a step over 4 times the input's largest step within 2 ms, or 0.05. Levels move by at most 0.08 dB, and the peak still never exceeds the input's.
- **On a held bass,** the crossfade between two phases of the note adds their difference over 96 frames to each step. The worst step is then 2.4 times the bass's own largest at 120 bpm and 2.7 times at 133: a 2 ms ramp, under the click threshold.

### Switching (our call)

A restart that cuts off Rush, such as a program switch, lets what Rush was playing run on as a tail under what follows, as Tape's motions do.
- **The crossfade.** Over 5 ms, the output fades linearly from the tail to the new program. It starts wholly on the tail.
- **The tail continues Rush's sound** (`Cutter::rushSound`):
  - in a cut, the cut from its next frame;
  - on a join, the cut that just ended, past its end;
  - once a block has run out (its track, or a roll's run-on), the input itself, which is what Rush plays next.
- **Why.** Spliced, a switch in the middle of a roll put the repeat straight against the next program's first cut: on the processor's test sine, Rush → Tape stepped 4.2 times the larger of the two programs played throughout. And a switch on one of Rush's block boundaries (its beats and bar lines, where the pad quantises switches) found no cut to fade: the next program's first sample was exact silence, in 22 to 37% of the review's beat-quantised switches, with steps up to 0.70 on a held bass.
- **Into Rush.** A restart whose next procedure is Rush lets the last Classic program's cut run on the same way (on a cut boundary, the cut that just ended); a silent cut fades Rush in from silence. Tape keeps its own tail. Only restarts into Rush take it.
  - **This fade mixes before the effects,** so the outgoing program's effects shape it: Filter's and Suction's filter, which Impact holds as it ramps out over 2 ms. Mixed after them, as first built, a switch from a filtered program jumped to the raw cut: on the beat, Filter → Rush stepped 0.42 on a held bass and 0.59 on a pad chord, Suction → Rush 0.93 and 1.06. Before, with a splice and no fade, they were 0.10, 0.25, 0.27 and 0.25. Now they are 0.004, 0.026, 0.009 and 0.036, and Cut, Bounce and Glitch → Rush stay at 0.007 to 0.008 on the bass (0.59 to 0.68 spliced).
  - **From a silent cut** the fade reads the frame of the switch at zero gain, so Rush's track rises from silence over 5 ms (largest step 0.003 on a held bass, 0.74 before).
- **Only Rush's restarts take a tail,** so no other program's switch changes.
- **Measured** (review harness, X 0.8, every Y, switches to all six programs on every beat of a bar, at 97, 120, 133 and 174 bpm, 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz): exact silence in 85 to 140 of 384 switches before, **0** after. Where a tail fades, the worst step is 0.005 to 0.012 on the bass and 0.012 to 0.028 on the pad chord, against their own 0.0043 and 0.026.

### Classic's settings at the end of a bar

A switch to the Classic surface lands Classic's procedure on the next bar line. Until then Rush plays on with Classic's settings, which never roll, so it plays the track. A Rush plan takes no accent and no transform, so Classic's accent, Attack Align and reverse don't reach that track; with an accent of 1 they had taken up to 2.5 dB off the last beat.

## Pad

- `isAvailable (rush)` is true. Captions: X "Push", Y "Roll" **(our call)**.
- **Mapping, with x and y in [0, 1]:**
  - procedure `rush`, 16 units per bar, seed 808 (unused: Rush draws nothing). Engaging lands on the next of the bar's 16 units: a 16th note in 4/4, 3/16 of a beat in 3/4, 7/32 in 7/8;
  - `rollCurve = 2x − 1`;
  - `rollCents = 500 (2x − 1)`, a fourth up at the right and down at the left **(our call)**;
  - `rollBeats = {1, 2, 3, 4}[min (⌊4y⌋, 3)]`, four equal steps with no hysteresis;
  - the base's other `CutParameters`. Straight comes from the pad's switch, as for every program.
- **There are no discrete zones,** so moving the pad never restarts the cutting. Changes take effect from the next block: the roll's start, or the bar line.
- The pad page gives Rush a tooltip in the voice of the others, and the README gets one sentence.
- The curves are starting points, tuned by ear. The verdict goes in the listening audit.

- **RED/GREEN tests.**
- **Pinned first.** Before any engine change:

  - extend the 97 bpm event hash (a restart, an idle toggle and two tempo changes, a bar that isn't whole samples) with Tape, and with Classic's CutProc11, WarpCut, SQPusher (Straight) and Bounce at fades of 0 and 2 ms, pitched and panned, measured on the unchanged engine.

  These, `LegacyEquivalenceTests` and every existing hash stay unchanged. Rush joins both hashes as built, so the next slice can show it unchanged.
- **The review's fuzz.** Base (this branch's parent) against head, bitwise, over 400 random scenarios: 8, 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz, meters 4, 3, 3.5, 2.5 and 6, every Classic procedure and Fracture family, Cut, Bounce, Glitch, Suction, Filter and Tape, with restarts, idle, tempo changes, seeks, `resetRendering` and random partitions. Measured: 172.7 M frames, 9,605 events, **0 mismatches**. With one deliberate change to the fade (a mutation), it finds 18 mismatches in 30 scenarios.
- **Rush's own fuzz.** 60 random scenarios at 8, 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz, in eight meters, with random spots, Straight, restarts, idle, tempo steps and seeks: one-frame, random and 1,024-frame partitions are bitwise identical, with no NaN, no cutter error, and a peak never above the input's. 30 of them are also clean with address and undefined-behaviour sanitizers and assertions on.
- **The layout (pure), over x and y, meters 4, 3, 5, 7, 2.5, 3.5, 6.5, 1, 0.5 and 0.25, and every unit to engage on:**
  - Every block keeps the procedure contract. Rush draws nothing from the random generator.
  - The roll ends exactly on the bar line: the cuts sum to the bar within 10⁻⁹ units.
  - Engaged from the bar line, the roll starts `min (rollBeats, B)` before the bar line, has `round (4T)` cuts, and the bar's other block is one track cut.
  - The first-to-last ratio is `8^c` within 10⁻⁹, with every cut shorter than the one before in a rush, longer in a drag, and equal at the centre.
  - No roll cut is shorter than 1/32 beat. Over every meter from ⅛ to 8 beats in eighths, the shortest is 1/24 of a beat (3/8 of a beat in two cuts, 8:1).
  - Engaged late, the roll starts on the next whole beat before the bar line, never before the engage.
  - Pitch and level: cut 0 at 0 cents and unity, the last cut at `rollCents` and unity, the repeats even in decibels from −12 dB.
  - `rollBeats` 0 gives one track cut to the bar line.
  - With Straight, every cut is a whole number of 64ths of a beat, and the step ratio is `2^m`.
- **Through the Cutter:**
  - **The track, bitwise.** At every whole-number tempo from 80 to 180 bpm, at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz, over four bars in 4/4, Rush is the input outside its rolls' repeats and their 2 ms joins, sample for sample.
  - **Half-sample ties.** At every whole-number tempo from 80 to 180 bpm at 44.1 kHz where a bar line or a roll's start ties, in 4/4, 3/4, 5/4, 7/8, 5/8 and 13/8, over five bars: the track bitwise, rolls landing, and the same output in host buffers of 1, 37, 512 and 4096 frames. 128 bpm in 4/4 is its own test.
  - **Rolls land.** At those tempos and in 3/4, 5/4, 7/4, 5/8 and 13/8, each new bar starts within half a sample of the host's bar line, each roll plays all its cuts, and its last cut ends within a frame of the bar line. Through tempo ramps of ±0.5 and ±3 bpm a beat, with the tempo changing every 1, 64, 512 or 2048 frames, within 4 frames either side.
  - **Joins.** On a held bass and a pad chord at 120 and 133 bpm, at 20 spots with Straight on and off, no step is over 3 times the material's own largest (measured 2.7 on the bass, 1.3 on the chord; spliced, 150 to 207 and 27 to 35).
  - **Switches on the beat.** Switching out of Rush to Cut, Tape and Filter on every beat of a bar, the bar line included, at four roll lengths, on a held bass and a pad chord at 120 and 133 bpm and on drums: no exact silence, and every step within 1.5 times the larger of the two programs played throughout and the input, in the processor's ±2400-sample window (measured 1.25).
  - **The floor.** At every tempo from 60 to 240 bpm in steps of 6, in 4/4, 3/4 and 5/8, no roll cut is under 30 ms, a rush still rushes and a drag drags, Straight stays on the 64th grid, and at 120 bpm or slower every layout is the one without the floor. Through the cutter at 180 bpm, Straight's whole-bar rush never cuts under 30 ms.
  - No frame drops to silence.
  - **Arrangement loops.** A two-bar loop that wraps on its bar line, in 4/4 and 3/4 at 93 and 120 bpm, lands each roll on the loop's end and opens the next pass on the track, exactly.
  - **A restart mid-roll** crossfades from the repeat to what follows over 5 ms, within 10⁻⁶ of the weighted sum of the two. It steps no more than the repeat or the input do around it.
  - **Arrangement loops** cross a roll's last repeat into the loop's first sample over 2 ms, then play the track exactly.
  - **Pitched repeats play to their end.** Every roll cut's playable samples equal its sounding samples.
  - **On drums** (the review's loop, alone, with a 55 Hz sub and with a 150 Hz bass), at 5 × 4 spots:
    - the peak never exceeds the input's;
    - with a bass line, the RMS is never more than 0.25 dB over the input's (measured +0.05 dB);
    - on drums alone, never more than 2.5 dB over the input's (measured +2.35 dB), and never more than 1 dB over Cut's busiest spot on the same loop (measured +0.8 dB);
    - never more than 3.5 dB under the input at any spot (measured −3.4 dB).
    - Without the swell, the drums alone rise 5.8 dB and the bars fail.
- **Processor:**
  - **Engage is seamless.** From the touch until the first roll, the output equals the input, bitwise.
  - **Engage, release and switch** on Rush leave the cutter error-free. Release returns exactly to the input.
  - **Switch clicks.** Within ±2400 samples of the switch, against the larger of the two programs played throughout in the same window, switching in the middle of a whole-bar roll:
    - Rush → Cut and Cut → Rush: at most 0.05× (measured 0.012×; Cut → Rush spliced was 1.7×);
    - Rush → Tape and Tape → Rush: at most 0.5× (measured 0.31× and 0.30×; Rush → Tape spliced was 4.2×);
    - Glitch → Rush: at most 1.25× (measured 1.0×; spliced, 1.34×);
    - Filter and Suction → Rush on the beat, at 9 spots each, on a held bass and a pad chord: at most 2× in the engine's ±2400-sample window (measured 1.8× on the bass, 0.012, and 1.0× on the chord; mixed after the effects, 125× and 26×);
    - from a silent cut into Rush: the largest step under 0.01 (measured 0.003);
    - Rush → Classic, around the bar line where Classic's procedure takes over, before the roll and during it: at most 1× (measured 0.36× and 0.75×).

  - **An arrangement loop** through the processor's epoch path opens on the track.
  - **Determinism.** Engaging at the same spot in the same bar after a different history gives bit-identical output.
  - **Every buffer partition** gives identical output, at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz, and at 128 bpm and 44.1 kHz, where every bar line ties.

## Known limits

- **A roll on sustained material is a run of joins.** Each repeat restarts the hit, and the bar line hands back to the track. Each join now crossfades over 2 ms, so a held note doesn't click, but it still restarts: a held bass in a roll is a pulse of restarts, as in a stutter roll. On drums the restart is the hit's own attack, and 0.3 repeats a bar still step over 4 times the local input (from 3.5), where a repeat restarts in a loud part of a hit.
- **The downbeat after a roll isn't the input for 2 ms,** and each repeat's attack ramps in over 2 ms. The owner accepted both for click-free joins.
- **On a held bass the crossfades are a little steeper than the note,** at most 2.7 times its own largest step at 133 bpm: a 2 ms ramp between two phases of the note, under the click threshold of 4 times.
- **The swell's first repeat (by ear).** It is 12 to 13.7 dB under the hit at every spot. Against what the input would have played at that moment, on drums alone it is anywhere from 21 dB under (X 0.75 to 1, a three-beat roll) to 4 dB over; at worst 15.6 dB under with a sub and 14.0 dB with a 150 Hz bass. In a long rush the first repeat is long: a three-beat rush at X 1 has a hit of 0.58 beat and a first repeat of 0.48 beat (240 ms at 120 bpm), so the hollow is about a quarter note that replaces the and-of-2 and beat 3. On drags the quiet repeats are the short early ones. If the ear finds it hollow, the swell could start at −6 dB for rushes, or swell by time rather than by cut.
- **The low end on repeats (by ear).** Below 120 Hz, over each roll's span, against the input over the same span: with a 55 Hz sub, at most +0.4 dB RMS and +0.9 dB peak; with a 150 Hz bass, up to +3.8 dB RMS and +3.6 dB peak on short drags at X 0; on drums alone up to +5.7 dB RMS but +1.9 dB peak, because a rolled kick fills the gaps between kicks. If it's heard as thumping, the repeats could be high-passed gently, or a kick's swell held lower for longer.
- **The source is a beat, not a detected hit.** In 4/4 the beats are where kicks and snares sit, but a roll in music with nothing on the beat repeats whatever is there. In meters of eighths it starts on an off-beat eighth.
- **Late engages.** Engaging after the roll should have started gives a shorter roll from the next beat, or none if the bar's last beat has begun. From the next bar on, every roll is the full length.
- **Pitch is resampling,** with linear interpolation, as the Classic pages pitch: at most a fourth either way.
- **Above 120 bpm the 30 ms floor flattens the fastest rolls.** A fast rush keeps its direction but loses some of its curve, or Straight a halving. Straight loses its 64ths above 125 bpm. Only above 500 bpm would an even roll have fewer cuts than 16ths.
- **Classic and the other programs keep a one-frame fade and their dropped frame** at a block that ends early. Changing that would change their bits.
- **A jump in the host's position** (a seek, or a loop whose end isn't a bar line) cuts a roll where it is, crossfaded over 2 ms from what it was playing; the host's own audio jumps there too.
- **Through tempo ramps a roll lands within 0.1 ms,** not within a frame: see the run-on above.
- **At 44.1 kHz, a restart on a 16th that ties half a sample can land a sample apart, depending on the host's buffer size.** This predates Rush, as Filter's spec records, and so do the pad's own switch and release points at a tie. Rush's own blocks start on whole beats and bar lines, and its tie rule settles those.
