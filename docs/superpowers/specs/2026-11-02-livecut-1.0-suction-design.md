# Livecut 1.0: Suction

Date: 2026-11-02. This is the first of three PRs that together make pad slice 2. The owner chose the split after the engine survey showed each slice 2 program needs new engine work. They chose "Split, Suction first". Calls made here rather than by the owner are marked **(our call)**.

The parent spec is [the pad design](2026-11-01-livecut-1.0-pad-design.md). It describes Suction as:
- X: band centre, low to high.
- Y: squeeze, where the high-pass and low-pass close in while resonance rises.
- The filter breathes with each cut, and its level is held so it never thins.

## What you hear

- **Bottom of the pad: open.** The band runs from 30 Hz to 18 kHz, so you hear the cuts unfiltered at any X.
- **Raising Y squeezes.** Both edges close in toward the band centre that X picks (120 Hz at the left to 5 kHz at the right), and the edges grow more resonant. The top of the pad is a narrow, singing band about 1½ octaves wide.
- **Every cut breathes.** At each cut the band opens 1½ octaves wider, then sucks closed over 180 ms. This pumps with the cutting rhythm, and gives Suction its name **(our call)**.
- **Level held.** Narrowing the band doesn't make it quieter. Its loudness tracks the unfiltered cuts.
- **Cutting underneath.** CutProc11 at 16 units per bar, with gentle re-editing: stutter chance .25, stutter area .5, at most one repeat. Seed 404. The filter is the star and the cuts give it a pulse **(our call)**.

## Engine

### A band filter in Impact

`ImpactParameters::Filter::Type` gains `band`, appended after `highPass`. The `impactFilterType` parameter keeps its three choices, so Classic can never select `band` and its automation doesn't change.

- New fields: `bandLowHz` and `bandHighHz`, each in [30, 18000], defaulting to 30 and 18000. They are used only by `band`.
- **Signal path.** A Simper SVF high-pass at the low edge, then a second SVF low-pass at the high edge. Both use the filter's `q`. The existing single-SVF types are unchanged sample for sample.
- **Breathing.** `envelopeOctaves × AD` opens both edges outward by that many octaves: the low edge moves down and the high edge moves up. `repeatOctaves × repeatProgress` shifts both edges together. Each edge is clamped to [30, 0.45 × sample rate]. The edges never come closer than a semitone, so the high edge stays at least a semitone above the low edge.
- **Smoothing.** In `band` mode, the two edges and `q` each follow their targets with a 20 ms one-pole. The edges are smoothed in log frequency. This stops zipper noise when the pad moves, since its changes land once per block. The other types are not smoothed, so Classic is unchanged.
- **Level hold.** In `band` mode, the filter's output is scaled by the ratio of two RMS followers: the filter's input and its output, each with a 150 ms time constant.
  - The gain is clamped to [−6, +18] dB and fades smoothly to 0 dB as the input follower drops through −80 to −86 dBFS, so silence is never amplified and the gain never steps.
  - The gain is a pure function of the audio, so it's deterministic.
  - Level matching is the standard way to compensate a band-limiting filter for loudness **(our call)**.
- The second filter's state and the followers are cleared in `reset()` and whenever the filter's wet mix is 0, like the existing state.

### The rack forgets when the pad goes idle

**Today:**
- When the pad releases, the gate fades to the input and the cutter goes idle.
- The effect rack (filter, resonator, frequency) then stops running with its state frozen.
- The next press resumes that stale ringing.

**Change:** when the cutter enters idle, it resets the rack, as `resetRendering()` already does.
- The pad gain is already 0 by then, so the reset is inaudible.
- A program restart while engaged doesn't reset the rack, because it doesn't go through idle.
- This fixes every program, not just Suction.

## Pad

- `PadMapping` gains `ImpactParameters impact`. `padSettings` copies it into `Settings::impact`, and Cut, Bounce and Glitch keep the default, Impact off.
- `isAvailable (suction)` is true. Captions: X "Band", Y "Squeeze".
- **Mapping, with x and y in [0, 1]:**
  - centre `c = 120 × (5000 / 120)^x` Hz;
  - squeeze `s = 0.85 y`;
  - `bandLowHz = 30^(1−s) · c^s`;
  - `bandHighHz = 18000^(1−s) · c^s`;
  - `q = 0.707 + 1.8 y`;
  - `envelopeOctaves = 1.5`;
  - `attackMs = 1`, `decayMs = 180`;
  - filter enabled, `chance = 1`, `mix = 1`.
- There are no discrete zones, so moving the pad never restarts the cutting.
- The curves are starting points, tuned by ear. The verdict goes in the listening audit.

## Quality gates

- **RED/GREEN tests:**
  - Band edges: −3 dB points within a third of an octave of `bandLowHz` and `bandHighHz` at `q` 0.707, measured with sines at 48 kHz.
  - Breathing: the edges sit wider at a cut's start than 180 ms later.
  - Level hold: on pink noise, output RMS stays within ±1.5 dB of input after 500 ms, from fully open to fully squeezed.
  - Smoothing: a step in an edge moves the output's spectrum gradually, with no output jump larger than the same render without the step.
  - Classic filter types are bit-identical to before.
  - Rack reset on idle.
  - The Suction mapping: ranges, bottom-of-pad open, determinism.
  - Engage, release and program switch on Suction leave the cutter error-free.
  - No allocation in the callback.
- pluginval strictness 10 and auval in CI.
- **By ear in Live (owner):** the whole pad, with no mud or thinning, and no clicks on engage, release or switch.

## Known limits

- The level hold follows loudness over about 150 ms, so a fast squeeze can briefly swell or dip before it settles.
- A program switch onto a coarser grid still passes the input until the new grid's next unit (existing behaviour). The filter's tail ends there.
