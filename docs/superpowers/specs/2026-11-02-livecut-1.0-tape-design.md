# Livecut 1.0: Tape

Date: 2026-11-02. This is the third of the three PRs that make pad slice 2, stacked on [Filter](2026-11-02-livecut-1.0-filter-design.md). The owner asked for the details to be decided here ("build what you think is best… canonical, modern, idiomatic"). Calls made here rather than by the owner are marked **(our call)**.

The parent spec is [the pad design](2026-11-01-livecut-1.0-pad-design.md). It describes Tape as:
- X: stop length, from half a beat to four beats.
- Y: how often stops happen, from phrase ends only up to every beat.
- Stops land on the grid and spin back up into the next hit.
- Engine: TapeCut.

## What you hear

- **Between stops, the track as it is.** Tape plays the input in place, at its own speed, untouched. Engaging Tape changes nothing you can hear until the first stop.
- **A stop brakes the tape to a halt on the grid.** The pitch holds, then falls away, and the sound fades to silence exactly on a beat or a bar line.
- **The next hit spins back up.** The cut on the grid point after a stop starts from silence, winds up through the octaves in a few milliseconds, overshoots a little and settles back exactly onto the track, all within a 32nd note. From there you hear the track as it is again, in time.
- **X: how long a stop is.** Half a beat, one beat, two beats or four beats **(our call: four steps, the lengths the Classic Tape page offers)**.
- **Y: how often.** At the bottom, one stop at the end of every four bars. Going up, the stops come every two bars, then every bar, then every two beats, then every beat. Between those steps the next finer one fades in, on a fixed choice of bars, so moving Y up adds stops one at a time **(our call)**.
- **Stops leave room.** A stop is never longer than half the time since the stop before it. At the top of the pad, every beat brakes for its second half, whatever X says.
- **Never louder.** A stop is slower and quieter than the track, and the spin-up never lifts the level, so Tape is never louder than the input.
- **Switching away lets the tape go.** A switch in the middle of a stop fades the slowed tape out under the next program over 5 ms, rather than cutting it off **(our call)**.

## Engine

### Why the cuts must never splice (our call)

Classic Tape replays its block's first frame at the start of every cut, and every cut is a stop. On the pad that would jump back in the music at every cut. The survey's recommendation was:
- normal cuts at speed 1, each reading from its own start frame;
- a stop at selected cuts;
- a spin-up on the next hit that starts silent;
- then the next cut reading fresh, which leaves a splice of about half the spin-up behind the track.

This design keeps the first three and removes the splice:
- **Every Tape cut reads from its own first frame.** A cut at speed 1 is then exactly the input. A stop starts exactly where the track is.
- **The spin-up catches up.** Its speed rises from 0, overshoots to 1.25 and lands at 1 exactly on the track, with no acceleration left. So the cut after it continues the same audio at the same speed.
- **So no Tape cut ever splices.** Each one starts where the last one ended, on the same frame at the same speed, or from the silence a stop ends in.
- **No fade (our call).** The user's lean was a 2 ms cut fade for Tape. A fade only helps at a splice, and Tape has none. At every join it would carve a 2 ms notch into continuous audio. Even the engine's 0 ms setting (a one-frame fade) zeroes each cut's first sample. Tape's cuts get no fade envelope at all.

### Per-cut motion

`Cut` gains `TapeMotion tape`, in `Cut.h`:
- `cycle`: the default, today's Classic Tape. The cut reads from its block's first frame, with the page's speeds, curve and Alternate, under the cut's fade.
- `play`: speed 1.
- `stop`: speed `1 − u²`, from 1 to 0.
- `spinUp`: the catch-up curve, from 0 to 1.

`play`, `stop` and `spinUp` are the pad's **motions**:
- Each reads from the render frame of its own first sample.
- Each is unpanned at unit level, with gate 1. The layout below sets them that way.
- Each has gain `√min (speed, 1)` with no fade envelope.
- Attack Align and Reverse don't apply. A transform would move the read away from the track, and the joins would splice.

The field is one byte in `Cut`'s padding, so `Cut` doesn't grow, and every existing procedure leaves it at `cycle`. Classic Tape's output is unchanged to the bit, pinned by a hash taken before the change.

### The brake (our call)

`TapeTrajectory` gains `Curve::quadratic`: shape `u²`, so a stop's speed is `1 − u²`.
- Its position is the exact discrete sum, like the existing curves.
- The pitch holds at first: −1 semitone a quarter of the way in, −5 halfway. Then it falls away into the grid point.
- **Why not linear or smoothstep.** On steady material, the share of a stop's energy heard below quarter speed (two octaves down, where it turns to rumble) is:
  - linear: 6.3%;
  - smoothstep, Classic's default: 5.8%;
  - quadratic: **2.6%**. It spends 13% of the stop there, against 25% and 33%.

  That is the mud the owner ruled out, so the quadratic brake is the cleanest of the three. It is also the "quadratic slowdown" the user suggested.
- **Gain `√speed`, as the engine's tape already uses.** The level falls to silence with the speed. The last sample of a stop is speed 0, exact silence, on the grid point.

### The spin-up (our call)

`TapeTrajectory` gains `Curve::catchUp`, for `from` 0 and `to` 1 only.
- With `u = n / T` over the cut's `T` frames:
  - position `p (n) = T · (3u² − 3u³ + u⁴)`;
  - speed `v (n) = 6u − 9u² + 4u³`.
- **What it guarantees:**
  - `p (0) = 0` and `v (0) = 0`: it starts still and silent.
  - `p (T) = T` and `v (T) = 1`, with zero slope: it lands exactly on the track, at the track's speed, with no acceleration left.
  - **It never reads ahead of the input.** `p (n) = n − n (1 − u)³ ≤ n`, so the head never passes the newest recorded frame. That makes it causal in Live mode, with no lookahead.
  - Its lag behind the track is at most 10.5% of `T`, at `u = ¼`.
  - Its speed reaches ½ at `u ≈ 0.1` and 1 at `u = ¼`. It peaks at 1.25 (+3.9 semitones) at `u = ½`, then settles.
- **Position is the closed form, not a discrete sum.** A sum of the speeds lands half a sample short. The closed form lands exactly, so the cut after it reads the next frame. The two agree within `O (1 / T)` of a frame.
- **Gain is `√min (speed, 1)`.** The overshoot is a pitch, never a level.
- **Its length is ⅛ beat (a 32nd note), as the user suggested.** That is 62.5 ms at 120 bpm:
  - the speed passes half within 6 ms and reaches 1 at 16 ms;
  - the overshoot is spread over the remaining 47 ms.
  - Shorter is only a fade-in. Longer smears the downbeat's hit and stretches the overshoot into a heard detune.
  - It must still be checked by ear.
- **Why it can't land on the hit itself.** In Live mode there's no future audio. A head that reaches the track at the grid point at speed ≤ 1 would have to start ahead of it. So the hit itself winds up, as the survey found.

### Tempo changes

`retimeTape` follows each motion's own end and curve:
- `play` stays at 1;
- `stop` brakes from its current speed to 0 over what's left.

A `spinUp` isn't re-timed. It runs its `T` frames, then plays on at speed 1, exactly on the track.
- If the tempo slows mid spin-up, the cut is longer, and it simply continues on the track.
- If the tempo rises, the cut can end before it lands. That leaves a splice of at most 10.5% of a 32nd note. This is a known limit.

### Switching away (our call)

A restart that cuts off a Tape motion, such as a program switch, lets the motion play on as a tail under what follows.
- **The crossfade.** Over 5 ms, the pad's release time, the output fades linearly from the tail to the new program.
  - It starts wholly on the tail, which continues the sample before the switch.
  - The new program is wholly in after 5 ms.
- **Mid-stop.** The slowed tape fades out rather than splicing onto the new program's first cut.
  - Spliced, a switch in the middle of a stop stepped 0.99 on a sine. That is 75 times the largest step of either program played throughout the same window.
  - Faded, it steps 1.01 to 1.11 times.
- **On the beat a stop ends.** The tail is the stop's silence, so the new program fades in rather than starting hard.
- **It never allocates.** The tail, `TapeTail`, is plain data, taken from the reader at the restart.
- **Only Tape takes one.** Only a Tape motion takes a tail, so no other program's switch changes.
- **Pad → Classic doesn't restart.** Tape's block runs to its bar line, and Classic's family takes over there.

### Where stops go

`FractureParameters::Tape` gains:
- `Layout layout`: `cycle` (the default, today's) or `stops`.
- `float stopBeats` (default 1): the stop length X asks for, in beats.
- `float stopDensity` (default 0, range [0, 4]): how often.

`chooseFractureBlock` with `stops` lays out the block (the rest of the bar, as for Classic Tape) through a pure function, `layOutTapeStops`, in `Fracture.cpp`.

**Candidate ends.** These are the beat boundaries inside the bar (whole quarter notes) and the bar line. Each has a level:
- **Level 0:** the end of a host bar `b` with `(b + 1) mod 4 = 0`, the last bar of every four, counted from the song's first bar;
- **level 1:** the end of any other even bar;
- **level 2:** any other bar line;
- **level 3:** an even beat inside the bar;
- **level 4:** any other beat.

**Phrase ends (our call).** The parent spec says "phrase ends only" for the bottom of Y. Livecut's own phrases are 1 to 2 bars on the pad, drawn at random. Here a phrase is four bars of the host, counted from bar 1:
- that is where arrangements breathe;
- it is the same for every engage.

**Selection.** The end at host bar `b`, beat `t` stops when `fractureDraw ({seed, b, 0, t, 0, 1}, tag) < clamp (stopDensity + 1 − level, 0, 1)`.
- So at density 0 only level 0 stops. At density 4 every candidate does.
- In between, the next level fades in on a fixed set of bars.
- The draw depends only on the seed, the host bar and the beat. So the same spot in the same bar always stops the same way, however and whenever you engaged.

**Length.** `L = min (stopBeats, B, gap / 2)`:
- `B` is the bar's length in beats;
- `gap` is the distance back to the latest selected end in the two bars before it, or `2B` if there is none.

So a stop always leaves at least half its own length of track before it, and never reaches the previous stop's spin-up. At density 4 in 4/4 every stop is half a beat. At density 2 (every bar) they're at most two beats.

**Layout of the block** from beat `s` to the bar line `e`:
1. If the previous block ended in a stop, the block opens with a `spinUp` of ⅛ beat.
2. For each selected end `t` in `(s, e]`, in order:
   - **Skip it** if its stop would start before the end of what's already laid out. That only happens when the pad engaged after the stop should have begun. A late engage never gets a shortened, faster brake.
   - **Otherwise** a `play` runs to `t − L`, then a `stop` runs to `t`.
   - If `t < e`, a `spinUp` of ⅛ beat follows.
   - If `t = e`, the next block opens with the spin-up.
3. A `play` fills the rest to `e`. The last cut takes the exact remainder, so the cuts sum to the block.

**State.** The "previous block ended in a stop" flag is the Cutter's only new state:
- it is cleared on `resetRendering ()` and on every drop (restart, idle, seek);
- a fresh engage or a switch into Tape therefore starts on the track, never with a spin-up;
- a Classic block clears it too.

**Straight.** Every boundary is on the ⅛-beat grid: stop lengths of ½, 1, 2 and 4 beats, halved gaps of whole beats, and ⅛-beat spin-ups. So every cut starts on a 64th of a beat, and Straight needs nothing more.

## Pad

- **`PadMapping` gains `FractureParameters fracture`**, and `padSettings` copies it into `Settings::fracture`. The other programs keep the default, Classic, so their settings don't change.
- **`isAvailable (tape)` is true.** Captions: X "Length", Y "Stops" **(our call)**.
- **Mapping, with x and y in [0, 1]:**
  - `stopBeats = {½, 1, 2, 4}[min (⌊4x⌋, 3)]`, four equal steps with no hysteresis. They are a pure function of x, and changing them never restarts the cutting;
  - `stopDensity = 4y`;
  - fracture `tapeCut`, layout `stops`;
  - unitsPerBar 16, so engaging lands on the next 16th; seed 606; procedure CutProc11, unused by Tape's cuts;
  - phrases of 1 to 2 bars, the pad's base, which Tape's stops don't use.
- **There are no discrete zones,** so moving the pad never restarts the cutting. A restart in the middle of a stop would splice it to the track.
- **Changes take effect from the next block** (the next bar line), the engine's existing rule.
- The pad page gives Tape a tooltip in the voice of the others, and the README gets one sentence.
- The curves are starting points, tuned by ear. The verdict goes in the listening audit.

## Quality gates

- **RED/GREEN tests:**
  - **Pinned first.** Before any engine or mapping change:
    - a hash of Classic Tape and Classic Grain through the Cutter. The Tape cases use the cycle layout with smooth and linear curves, Alternate, half-beat, one-beat and four-beat cycles, and partial speeds;
    - a hash of Cut, Bounce, Glitch, Suction and Filter through the processor.

    They stay unchanged, as do `LegacyEquivalenceTests`, Classic's filter hashes and Suction's band hash.
  - **The brake.** `1 − u²` from 1 to 0, ending at exactly 0. Its position is the discrete sum of its speeds within 10⁻⁹.
  - **The catch-up:**
    - starts at speed and position 0;
    - peaks at 1.25 ± 10⁻⁶ at the midpoint;
    - never reads ahead of the frame it renders;
    - lands with `p (T) = T` exactly, and speed within 10⁻⁶ of 1 on its last frame;
    - plays on at speed 1 after `T`.
  - **The layout (pure):**
    - At density 0, over 64 bars, stops end only at the bar line after bars 4k + 3.
    - At density 4, every beat ends a half-beat stop.
    - Each stop is `min (X, B, gap / 2)` long and ends on a beat or bar line.
    - Every stop is followed by a ⅛-beat spin-up, in its block or at the start of the next.
    - Cuts sum exactly to the block. Every boundary is a whole number of 64ths of a beat, which confirms Straight.
    - It holds in 3/4 and 7/8 too.
    - Engaging mid-bar gives the full bar's layout from the first stop that fits.
    - Raising the density only adds stops.
  - **On drums through the Cutter** (the review's loop: kick, snare, hats and a bass line), at 3 × 3 spots of the pad:
    - **Seamless.** Outside stops and spin-ups the output equals the input, bitwise.
    - **The stop's tail.**
      - It ends in exact silence on its grid point, a whole 32nd note.
      - Its last 5 ms step by at most 0.5% of the drums' own largest step (measured 0.34%).
      - Below quarter speed it carries at most 2% of the stop's energy (measured 1.5%).
    - **The spin-up.**
      - It starts from exact silence, and steps by at most 0.02 in its first millisecond (measured 0.016).
      - It is never steeper than the hit it plays: at most 1× the input's largest step over the same 32nd (measured 0.77×).
      - On its last frame it is within 10⁻⁶ of the input (measured 3 × 10⁻⁸). From its hand-back on, the output equals the input bitwise.
    - **Level.** These hold on drums alone, with a 55 Hz sub-bass, and with a 150 Hz bass line:
      - Tape's RMS is never above Cut's, nor the input's, and never more than 1 dB below the input's (measured −0.01 to −0.72 dB);
      - its peak never exceeds the input's.
  - **Processor:**
    - **Engage is seamless.** From the touch until the first stop, the output equals the input bitwise.
    - **Engage, release and switch** on Tape leave the cutter error-free. Release returns exactly to the input.
    - **Switch clicks.** Within ±2400 samples of the switch, against the larger of the two programs played throughout in the same window:
      - Tape → Cut and Tape → Filter in the middle of a four-beat stop: at most 1.25× (measured 1.01× and 1.11×);
      - Tape → Cut on the beat a stop ends: at most 1.25× (measured 0.31×);
      - Cut → Tape: at most 1.25× (measured 1.0×);
      - Tape → Classic in the middle of a stop, around the bar line where Classic takes over: at most 1× (measured 0.64×).
    - **In the engine,** a restart mid-stop steps no more than the stop or the input itself (measured 0.84×), then plays the track exactly.
    - **Determinism.** Engaging at the same spot in the same bar after a different history gives bit-identical output.
    - **Every buffer partition** gives identical output.
    - **44.1, 48 and 96 kHz** play stops and spin-ups error-free, and land them on their grid points.
    - **No allocation** in the callback.
  - **The Tape mapping:** ranges, determinism, the steps of X and the density of Y, and no restarts while moving.
- pluginval strictness 10 and auval in CI.
- **By ear in Live (owner):** the whole pad, with:
  - no clicks on engage, release, switch, stop or spin-up;
  - no rumble or mud in the tails;
  - spin-ups that read as winding back into the beat.

## Known limits

- **The hit after a stop winds up.** In Live mode the spin-up can't land on the hit, because that needs future audio. So the hit's first few milliseconds are slurred up from silence, and its body bends up to +3.9 semitones for a moment before it settles.
- **The bottom of Y counts phrases from the song's bar 1.** If a track's phrases start elsewhere, the stop lands on its fourth bar from bar 1, not from the phrase.
- **At the top of Y, X changes less.** Stops are capped at half the gap before them. At every beat they're all half a beat, and at every two beats at most one.
- **A switch into Tape is a splice** from the last program's cut onto the track, as any restart is. Measured, it is no larger than the two programs' own steps.
- **A switch to Classic waits for Tape's bar line.** There Classic's first cut starts hard, from a stop's silence if one ends there, as Classic's own cuts do.
- **Release mid-stop fades over the gate's 5 ms,** like every program.
- **A tempo rise during a spin-up** can end it before it lands, leaving a splice of at most 10.5% of a 32nd note.
- **At 44.1 kHz, a restart on an odd 16th can land a sample apart, depending on the host's buffer size.** This predates Tape, as Filter's spec records.
  - After the first, Tape's blocks start on bar lines, which are whole samples at 120 bpm.
  - The cuts inside a block are rounded from its start the same way for every buffer size.
- **A stop crawls through near-silent sub-audio before it ends.** The quadratic brake keeps that under 3% of a stop's energy on steady material.
