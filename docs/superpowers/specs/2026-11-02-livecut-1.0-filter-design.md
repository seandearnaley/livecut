# Livecut 1.0: Filter

Date: 2026-11-02. This is the second of the three PRs that make pad slice 2, stacked on [Suction](2026-11-02-livecut-1.0-suction-design.md). The owner asked for the details to be decided here ("build what you think is best… canonical, modern, idiomatic"). Calls made here rather than by the owner are marked **(our call)**.

The parent spec is [the pad design](2026-11-01-livecut-1.0-pad-design.md). It describes Filter as:
- X: cutoff.
- Y: resonance and tail.
- Tuned tails ring at the ends of rolls, and the mix ducks to stay clean.
- Engine: the Impact filter and the Resonator.

## What you hear

- **X sweeps a low-pass.** From dark and close at the left (200 Hz) to fully open at the right (18 kHz), on an exponential curve, so equal moves sound like equal steps.
- **Bottom of the pad: a plain filter.** No resonance and no tail. The bottom-right corner is the open band Suction starts from, so it sounds like the cuts unfiltered.
- **Raising Y makes it ring.** The low-pass grows resonant, squelching at its cutoff, and the ends of rolls start to ring on a tuned note.
- **Tuned tails at the ends of rolls.** When a roll ends, the next cut strikes a tail: a clean, decaying tone on the cutoff's own note, in a singing register. It's as loud as half the roll at the top of the pad, and rings up to 900 ms.
- **The tail ducks under the hits.** At every cut the tail dips 12 dB within 2 ms, then swells back in behind the hit over about 80 ms. So the hits stay clean, and the tail sings in the gaps, pulsing with the cuts **(our call)**.
- **Level held.** Resonance never makes the level jump, and the sweep keeps the open filter's loudness, as Suction's band does.
- **Cutting underneath.** CutProc11 at 16 units per bar, with no repeats of regular cuts, so every multi-cut block is a stutter roll. Stutter chance .5, stutter area .5, seed 505. Most phrases end in a roll, so most phrases end in a tail **(our call)**.

## Engine

### Why not the Resonator (our call)

The parent spec names the Resonator as the tail source. The survey and a closer look rule it out:

- **Its retune throws tails away.** Any change of root starts a new bank from silence and crossfades over 50 ms. A tail tied to a moving cutoff would be cut off every time X moved.
- **It's excited all the time.** It hears every cut, not just the ends of rolls. So tails would pile up under the cutting: the mud the owner ruled out.
- **Its level isn't bounded on music.** Its voices peak at 0 dB, so broadband material loses most of its level at high mix. To ring audibly on drums it would need about 28 dB more excitation. With that gain, a bass note on its pitch would ring about 20 dB louder than the drums do.
- **It's Classic's.** Gating its input per cut would change code that Classic runs.

Instead, the tail is a small engine part inside the Impact filter, struck once per roll with a level taken from the roll. Its level is bounded by the material's, it never piles up, and nothing in Classic changes.

### A sweep filter in Impact

`ImpactParameters::Filter::Type` gains `sweep`, appended after `band`. Like `band`, it's engine-only, and `impactFilterType` keeps its three choices.

- **Signal path.** A Simper SVF high-pass at 30 Hz with q 0.707 (Butterworth), then a second SVF low-pass at `cutoffHz` with the filter's `q`. This is the `band` path with its low edge fixed at 30 Hz and its own q, so the open sweep (`cutoffHz` 18000, q 0.707) is exactly the open band. The 30 Hz high-pass also keeps sub-sonic rumble out of the makeup.
- **Modulation.** `envelopeOctaves × AD` and `repeatOctaves × repeatProgress` move only the low-pass edge. The high-pass stays at 30 Hz. The low-pass edge is clamped to [30 × 2^(1/12), 0.45 × sample rate], so it stays at least a semitone above the high-pass. Filter uses neither modulation.
- **Smoothing.** As for `band`: the low-pass edge (in log frequency) and `q` follow their targets with a 20 ms one-pole.
- **Level hold.** As for `band`: `gain = min (2, √(P_open / P_s (w, q)))`. Here `w = log2 (min (lowPassEdge, 18000) / 30)`, from the effective (smoothed, modulated) edge.
  - `P_s (w, q)` is the power that pink noise keeps through the 30 Hz Butterworth high-pass and a low-pass `w` octaves above it, with resonance `q`.
  - It's integrated over log frequency when the engine is prepared, into a table the same size as the band's. `integratePinkPower` gains separate qs for its two edges. The band passes the same q to both, so its table doesn't change.
  - Along Filter's path the cap is never reached: the makeup runs from +5.3 dB (200 Hz, q 0.707) to −1.3 dB (18 kHz, q 1.86).
- **Switching it off.** `sweep` joins `band` in Suction's rule: when the filter is disabled while sounding, or its type changes into or out of a smoothed type while it sounds, the sounding filter keeps its last shape, tail included, while its wet ramps out over 2 ms at the next cut. The new filter comes in at a later cut.

### The tail

New fields in `Filter`, used only by `sweep`:
- `tailLevel`, in [0, 1], default 0: the tail's RMS at its strike, as a fraction of the roll's.
- `tailDecayMs`, in [50, 2000], default 600: its decay time to −60 dB.

**Strike.**
- A roll is a block of two or more cuts.
- The cut after a roll's last cut strikes the tail, on that cut's first sample, when:
  - `tailLevel > 0`;
  - the filter is active on that cut;
  - and the filter isn't being held for a switch.
- The strike sets the tail's peak to `A = tailLevel · √2 · RMS`.
  - RMS is the filter's own output (after makeup, before the tail and the wet mix), over both channels, across every sample of the roll's block.
  - So a sine tail has `tailLevel` times the roll's RMS. Silence strikes nothing, and the tail can never be louder than the roll that struck it allows.

**Tuning (our call).**
- The note is the target `cutoffHz` (not smoothed, so the same X always gives the same note), snapped to the nearest equal-tempered semitone at A4 = 440 Hz.
- It's then moved by whole octaves into C4 to B5 (261.6 to 987.8 Hz):
  - `n = round (12 · log2 (cutoffHz / 440))`;
  - while `n < −9`, add 12; while `n ≥ 15`, subtract 12;
  - `f = 440 · 2^(n / 12)`.
- So the tail rings the filter's own resonance, made long and put in tune. It always sits above the kick and bass and below the hats, so it can't muddy the low end or pierce.

**Voice.**
- One mono sine, the same in both channels.
- At a strike, the amplitude ramps linearly from where it is to `A` over 2 ms, then decays exponentially to −60 dB over `tailDecayMs`.
- A strike while the tail still rings changes the pitch with the phase continuous, so the waveform never jumps. A silent tail starts at phase 0, so a given strike sounds the same every time.
- Below 10⁻⁶ (−120 dBFS) the tail is silent: its amplitude becomes 0 and its phase resets.

**Duck.**
- At every cut start, struck or not, the tail's gain glides linearly to 0.25 (−12 dB) over 2 ms.
- It then recovers toward 1 with a 40 ms time constant.
- The duck is sample-accurate and set only by the cut grid, so it's deterministic and never follows a level.

**Mix.** The tail joins the filter's wet: `out = in + mix · (hold · lowPass + tail − in)`.

**State.** The tail and its roll measurement are cleared with the rest of the band state: in `reset()`, whenever the wet mix is 0, and on a switch into a smoothed type. The rack's reset on idle (from Suction) therefore starts each engage with no tail.

### Diagnostics

`tailHz`, `tailAmplitude` (before the duck) and `tailDuck`. They read 0, 0 and 1 after a reset.

## Pad

- `isAvailable (filter)` is true. Captions: X "Cutoff", Y "Ring" **(our call)**.
- **Mapping, with x and y in [0, 1]:**
  - cutoff `c = 200 · 90^x` Hz;
  - `q = 0.707 + 2.3 · y · (1 − 0.5 x)`, so resonance reaches 3.0 at the left and eases to 1.86 at the right, keeping the treble from getting shrill **(our call)**;
  - `tailLevel = 0.5 y`;
  - `tailDecayMs = 200 + 700 y`;
  - type `sweep`, filter enabled, `chance = 1`, `mix = 1`, `envelopeOctaves = 0`, `repeatOctaves = 0`. The cutoff stays where X puts it, and the tail's duck gives the pulse **(our call)**.
  - Cutting: CutProc11, 16 units per bar, `stutterChance = .5`, `stutterArea = .5`, `minRepeats = maxRepeats = 0`, seed 505.
- There are no discrete zones, so moving the pad never restarts the cutting.
- The curves are starting points, tuned by ear. The verdict goes in the listening audit.
- The pad page gives Filter a tooltip in the voice of the others. The README gets one sentence.

## Quality gates

- **RED/GREEN tests:**
  - **Suction pinned first.** Before any engine change, a hash of the band's output over a fixed render pins Suction to the bit. Classic's three filter hashes and `LegacyEquivalenceTests` stay unchanged.
  - **Edges.** At q 0.707, with the makeup divided out, the gain is −3 dB ±1 dB at `cutoffHz` (200, 2000 and 8000 Hz) and at 30 Hz. It's below −20 dB two octaves above the cutoff. This holds at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz.
  - **Level hold.** On pink noise, along Filter's own path (y from 0 to 1, at x 0, 0.5 and 1, no rolls), output RMS stays within ±1.5 dB of the open band's output, both settled and 500 ms after a jump to that spot from the open filter. The makeup's cap is never reached there.
  - **Pink-power table.** It matches its direct integral to within 0.05 dB across its range. A flat sweep keeps its width in octaves.
  - **Glide.** A cutoff jump moves the output with no step larger than 1.5 × the same render without the jump.
  - **Strikes.** The tail is struck on the first sample of the cut after a roll's last cut, and only then:
    - not after a single-cut block;
    - not on a roll's own cuts;
    - not with `tailLevel` 0;
    - not while the filter is held.
  - **Tuning.** `tailHz` is the folded note, exactly, for cutoffs across the pad. With silence after the roll, the output's zero crossings give that frequency within 0.5%, at 44.1, 48 and 96 kHz.
  - **Decay.** The amplitude falls 60 dB within 2% of `tailDecayMs`.
  - **Level.**
    - The struck peak is `tailLevel · √2 ·` the roll's RMS, within 10⁻⁹, and a roll of silence strikes nothing.
    - On pink noise with a roll ending every bar, at y 1, the output's RMS over each bar is within +1.5 dB of the same render without a tail.
  - **Duck.** At each cut the gain reaches exactly 0.25 after 2 ms, and is within 1% of `1 − 0.75 e^(−t / 40 ms)` after that.
  - **No steps.** The tail's own largest sample-to-sample step stays within `1.05 · A · (2π f / rate + 2 / rampSamples)`, through strikes, re-strikes at a new pitch and ducks. That's the sine's own slew plus the 2 ms ramps of its amplitude and its duck.
  - **Switching away while the tail rings.**
    - The engine runs this at full wet, to Cut's Impact (off), to Suction's band and to Classic's low-pass.
    - The output equals the input plus the ramp times the unswitched twin's wet difference, within 10⁻⁶.
    - In a window of ±2400 samples, the largest step is no more than 3 × the larger of the two programs' largest steps throughout.
  - **Processor switches:**
    - It switches while a tail rings, at full Y, from Filter to Cut, Filter to Suction, Suction to Filter, and Filter to Classic's low-pass.
    - Within ±2400 samples of the switch, the largest step is no more than 3 × the larger of the largest steps of the two programs played throughout in the same window.
  - **Engage, release and switch** on Filter leave the cutter error-free. The release returns exactly to the input.
  - **Determinism.** Engaging Filter at the same spot in the same bar after a different history gives bit-identical output.
  - **Every buffer partition** gives identical output.
  - **No allocation** in the callback.
  - **The Filter mapping:** ranges, the open bottom-right, determinism, no restarts while moving.
- pluginval strictness 10 and auval in CI.
- **By ear in Live (owner):** the whole pad, with no mud, harshness or thinning, no clicks on engage, release or switch, and the tails in tune with the material often enough to be musical.

## Known limits

- **The tail is in equal temperament at A440, not in the track's key.** Holding X still holds a note, so you pick the note by where you play. Some notes will be outside a track's scale.
- **The tail's level follows the roll's loudness, not its pitch content.** A roll with nothing near the tail's note still strikes it, as a struck resonator would.
- **The level hold assumes broadband material,** as Suction's does. At the far left, a closed low-pass is lifted by up to 5.3 dB, which a bass-heavy break may hear as boom.
- **The tail is mono.**
- **A program switch fades a ringing tail out over 2 ms at the next cut.** A switch onto a coarser grid still passes the input until the new grid's next unit (existing behaviour).
