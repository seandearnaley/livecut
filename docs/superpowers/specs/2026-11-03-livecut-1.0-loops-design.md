# Livecut 1.0: Loops

Date: 2026-11-03. This is the last of the eight programs, the third PR of pad slice 3, stacked on [Rush](2026-11-02-livecut-1.0-rush-design.md). After it, every program on the pad is built. The owner asked for the details to be decided here ("build what you think is best… canonical, modern, idiomatic"). Calls made here rather than by the owner are marked **(our call)**.

The parent spec is [the pad design](2026-11-01-livecut-1.0-pad-design.md). It describes Loops as:
- X: the outer loop, from one bar down to one beat.
- Y: depth, meaning loops nested inside that loop, each shorter.
- Every level snaps to real hits.
- Engine: new work.

The engine code waits for Rush's review fixes, which also change `Cutter.cpp`. This document and its plan are written against `pad/rush` at `a7721c2`, and name the places where Loops reuses what those fixes add.

## What you hear

- **The bar you're in, looped.** Press the pad and the loop is the stretch of track you are in: the bar, the half bar or the beat. It plays out as the track, then repeats from the next loop boundary. Nothing you hear changes until that first repeat.
- **Four passes, then the track again.** A phrase is four passes of the loop: the track once, then three repeats. The next phrase catches the track where it has got to and loops that. So with X at the left the track moves on every four bars, at the right every bar **(our call)**.
- **X: how long the loop is.** A bar, half a bar or a quarter of a bar, in three zones. In 4/4 that is a bar, two beats or a beat, as the parent spec asks **(our call: halves of the bar, so every loop tiles the bar in every meter)**.
- **Y: how deep it folds.** Four equal steps. Each step up adds one more level: a loop of the loop's first half, then of its first quarter, then of its first eighth. The levels fill the end of the phrase, so the loop folds in on itself into the next phrase, like a DJ's loop roll **(our call)**:

  | Y step | Pass 1 | Pass 2 | Pass 3 | Pass 4 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 0 | track | loop | loop | loop |
  | 1 | track | loop | loop | ½ ½ |
  | 2 | track | loop | ½ ½ | ¼ ¼ ¼ ¼ |
  | 3 | track | loop | ½ ½ | ¼ ¼ ⅛ ⅛ ⅛ ⅛ |

  "½ ½" is the loop's first half, twice. With a one-bar loop in 4/4 at the top of Y: a bar of track, the bar again, two beats twice, one beat twice, then four eighths, and the track again on the next bar line.
- **Every level starts on the loop's first hit.** Each level loops the start of the one before it, so every pass and every level opens on the same hit, found in the recorded audio. Where no hit is close to the grid, it opens on the grid.
- **The outer loop is always heard.** Pass 2 repeats the whole loop at every Y. Only passes 3 and 4 fold.
- **Never louder than the loop.** A nested level that repeats a loud attack is turned down so that it is no louder than the loop it is nested in. It is never turned up.
- **Seamless joins.** Every join between passes, levels and phrases crossfades over 2 ms. The crossfade falls in the 2 ms just before each repeat's first hit, so the hit itself is untouched.
- **Straight** changes nothing: every Loops cut is already an even division of the bar.
- **Deterministic.** Loops makes no random choices. The same spot in the same place in the bar loops the same audio the same way.
- **Switching and releasing let the loop go.** A release fades to the track on the beat, as for every program. A switch fades the loop out under the next program over 5 ms.

## Why prefix nesting, not offset nesting (our call)

The engine survey offered two shapes:
- **prefix nesting**: every level replays from the loop's start, so it needs no new read position;
- **true nesting**: a new `Cut::sourceUnits` lets each level loop any sub-span, for example the second half of the bar.

This design takes prefix nesting, for these reasons:
- **The first hit is the strongest place to loop.** A loop's start is a grid point that X chose (a bar line, a half bar or a beat), and it is where the snap looks for a hit. A level that starts in the middle of the loop starts on a weaker position, often an off-beat. On the review's drum loop, three of the four off-beat eighths hold only a hi-hat.
- **It is the gesture players know.** A loop roll that halves from the loop's start is how DJs fold a loop. Each level stays inside the one before it, which is what "nested" asks.
- **It keeps one read position per phrase.** Every replay in a phrase reads from the same snapped frame. So the snap is decided once, the level hold is measured once, and the existing read paths (tails, run-ons, availability) work unchanged.
- **`Cut` doesn't grow.** A `double sourceUnits` would make `Cut` 40 bytes and every block plan 2 KB bigger. Loops needs one byte, in `Cut`'s padding.

## Why its own snap, not Attack Align (our call)

The survey noted that prefix nesting "works today with Attack Align". Attack Align is the wrong tool here:
- **It centres on the block's first frame.** A Loops replay block starts a whole loop after the audio it replays.
- **It needs the whole span recorded when the cut starts.** So the first repeat, straight after the track pass, can snap only to hits before the grid.
- **Its cuts can't crossfade.** `LivePlayer::tail()` is empty under a transform.

Loops instead snaps the phrase's read position once, with `OnsetDetector::nearest`, and plays every replay as a plain block read from that position.

## Engine

### The procedure

`Procedure` gains `loops`, appended after `rush`. The `procedure` parameter keeps its four choices, so Classic can never select it. `Cutter::procedureFor` returns a new `Loops` (in `CutProcedures.h`, `Loops.cpp`).

`CutParameters` gains two fields, used only by Loops:
- `double loopBars = 0`: the outer loop as a fraction of the bar: 1, ½ or ¼. 0 plays no loop.
- `int loopDepth = 0`: the nested levels, 0 to 3.

With the defaults Loops plays the track. So if the parameters change to Classic's mid-phrase (a switch to the Classic surface), Loops plays the track to the bar line, and Classic takes over there.

`Cut` gains `std::uint8_t loopLevel = 0`, in its padding, so `Cut` stays 32 bytes (static-asserted):
- 0: not a replay. The cut reads its block's own first frame, as every cut does today.
- 1: the whole loop. 2: its first half. 3: its first quarter. 4: its first eighth.

`BlockContext` gains `bool loopCaptured = false`. The Cutter sets it once the phrase's loop is captured (below).

**Phrases are four passes:** `choosePhraseBars` returns `max (1, round (4 · loopBars))` without a draw: 4, 2 or 1 bars.

**Blocks.** With `U` units per bar, the loop is `Lu = U · loopBars` units: 16, 8 or 4 at 16 units per bar. These are whole units, and powers of two, so every boundary is exact in binary in every meter. That avoids the ulp drift Tape found in 5/4. With `k = ⌊unitsDone / Lu⌋`, the pass the block starts in:
- `loopBars` 0: one track cut to the bar line.
- Not yet captured, or starting off a pass boundary: one track cut to the end of the pass.
- Captured, on a pass boundary: pass `k`'s layout from the table above, for `D = loopDepth`. Each cut is `Lu / 2^ℓ` units at level `ℓ`, with `loopLevel = ℓ + 1`.
- Every cut has gate 1, unity, centre, no pitch and `fade` false. The track pass and the repeats are joined by the crossfade below, not by the fade envelope.
- Comparisons use a tolerance of 10⁻⁹ units.

**The floor (our call).** No cut is shorter than 1/16 of a beat. With `B` beats in the bar, the deepest level is `B · loopBars / 8` beats, which is ⅛ beat at a quarter-bar loop in 4/4 (62.5 ms at 120 bpm). Only bars under two beats (3/8, 2/8, 1/4) reach the floor. There the depth stops at the last level at or above it.

**Straight.** Every Loops cut is `B / 2^n` beats with `n ≤ 5`, so it is a whole number of 64ths of a beat in every meter whose bar is a whole number of eighths (4/4, 3/4, 5/4, 7/4, 5/8, 7/8, 13/8, 6/8, 12/8). The Straight gate holds without Straight doing anything, so Loops ignores it.

**Late engages.** The phrase starts on the unit boundary after the press (16 units per bar, so the next 16th in 4/4). Its first block is a track cut to the end of the pass it starts in. That pass is the capture. The passes after it play their roles from the table, by position. So:
- with a one-bar loop, the phrase always starts with the bar you pressed in, and the first repeat is at most a bar away;
- with shorter loops, a press late in the phrase skips the first passes. A press in the last pass of the phrase gets the track until the next phrase, which starts at most a pass later **(our call, as Rush's late rolls)**.

### The capture

A phrase captures one loop: `L` frames of input starting at the **capture frame** `c`.
- **The pass you press in.** The capture is the whole pass the phrase starts in, including the part before the press. The listener heard that part as the track, because the pad was idle or playing another program. So `c` is the frame of the pass's start: the track block's first frame, minus how far into the pass it starts.
  - That is `c = a − round (i · samplesPerUnit)`, where `a` is the frame the track block activates on and `i` the units into the pass. On a pass boundary `i` is 0, and `c` is exact.
  - The audio was recorded. The history holds 64 seconds, and `i · samplesPerUnit` is under a bar, so under 8 seconds.
- **Never across a transport jump.** If `c` falls before the current transport epoch's first frame (a seek, an arrangement loop or transport start in the middle of a pass), that pass doesn't capture. It plays the track, and the next pass, which starts in the epoch, captures instead.
- **After a restart** (an X zone change, a switch into Loops), the part before the restart was not the track: it was the previous loop or program. The capture still reaches back. The repeat then plays track audio at the right place in the bar that the listener didn't hear. That is "catch a new loop where the track is now" **(our call)**.

The capture is fixed at the track block's activation, so it is exact even where a bar line falls half a sample between frames (Rush's review Finding 1).

### Snapping to hits (our call)

At the first replay of a phrase, the Cutter chooses the **read position** `s`, which every replay in the phrase reads from.
- **Search.** `OnsetDetector::nearest` searches for the nearest marker to `c` within `±T`, with `T = min (20 ms, L / 4)`, in the capture's transport epoch, with a minimum rise of 3 dB. That is the detector's own sensitivity, so any marker it published counts.
  - 20 ms is Attack Align's radius.
  - `L / 4` keeps a snap well inside the loop. It binds only for loops under 80 ms, which only very short bars at fast tempos reach: a quarter of a 1/8 bar at 180 bpm is 42 ms, so ±10 ms.
- **Preroll.** `s = m − p`, where `m` is the marker's frame and `p = ⌈2 ms⌉` is Attack Align's preroll. With no marker within `±T`, `s = c − p`: the grid.
  - So every replay starts 2 ms before its hit, snapped or not. The hit sounds 2 ms after the grid point, as Attack Align's do.
  - `s` is clamped to the epoch's first frame. Only a capture at the very start of an epoch loses its preroll.
- **It never reads ahead of the recording.** Replays play at speed 1 from `s ≤ c + T`, and the first replay starts at `c + L`. So the read head is always at least `L − T ≥ 3L / 4` behind the recording.
  - The search window is analysed by then: the detector publishes a marker within a millisecond, and the window ends `L − T` before the lookup.
- **The track pass isn't snapped.** It is the input, and its hits are where they are.
- **Deterministic.** The lookup happens on a frame fixed by the grid. The detector's markers depend only on the input, so `s` is the same for every buffer partition.

**What the detector finds.** On the review's drum loop alone, every loop start in the prototype snapped. With a 55 Hz sub or a 150 Hz bass under it, the detector marks only one eighth in four (31 of 124), so most loops start on the grid. The grid start is still a hit when the music is on the grid, which the review's loop is.

### The level hold (our call)

A nested level repeats the start of the loop, which is usually its loudest part: a kick's attack. Repeated, it lifts the level, as Rush's rolls did. Unlike Rush, Loops can hold the level causally. The whole loop is recorded before its first replay, so the Cutter can measure it.
- **Measured once per phrase**, at the first replay, over the snapped span. The Cutter takes the RMS of the frames `[s, s + L / 2^ℓ)` for `ℓ` = 0, 1, 2, 3, in one pass over the loop.
  - The pass reads at most one bar of history, 96,000 frames for a 120 bpm bar at 48 kHz, once a phrase.
  - It doesn't allocate.
- **Gain** for level `ℓ`: `g_ℓ = clamp (rms₀ / rms_ℓ, −12 dB, 1)`. The whole loop has `g₀ = 1`, and a level is never turned up. A silent span takes gain 1.
- **Applied** to each replay cut's level when its block activates.

Measured with the prototype (the layouts rendered on arrays, with 2 ms joins), over 16 bars of the review's loop at 120 bpm, at 3 × 4 spots, against the input:

| Material | Without the hold | With the hold | Cut's busiest spot (Rush spec) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drums alone | 0.00 to **+3.64** dB | −0.01 to **+1.20** dB | +1.6 dB |
| Drums + 55 Hz sub | −0.14 to +0.78 dB | −0.15 to +0.45 dB | +0.8 dB |
| Drums + 150 Hz bass | 0.00 to +1.22 dB | −0.01 to +0.33 dB | +0.5 dB |

- **What's left is the loop itself.** The +1.2 dB is a half-bar loop of the first half of the review's bar, which holds two kicks to the second half's one. It is there at every Y.
- **The peak never exceeds the input's.** Every replay reads input at unity or below, and a linear crossfade of two signals never exceeds the larger.
- **The gains the hold sets** on the review's loop:
  - drums alone: 0 to −7.7 dB, the deepest at a beat loop's eighths;
  - with the sub: 0 to −4.1 dB;
  - on a sustained pad chord: within 0.1 dB of unity.
  - So sustained material is untouched, and only loud attacks are held.
- **Why not a fixed trim or a swell.** A fixed trim of 3 dB a level still leaves drums alone at +2.3 dB, and dips sustained material by 3 to 6 dB in the deep levels, where nothing needed holding. A Rush-style swell from −12 dB would make every fold a hollow on sustained material.

### Joins (our call)

Each repeat replays recorded audio from `s`, so every join is a splice: from the end of one pass or level to the start of the loop. On sustained material a splice is a step. The prototype measured spliced joins on a held 55 Hz + 110 Hz bass (0.6 and 0.15): the largest step is **0.598**, against the bass's own 0.0065. This is Rush's Finding 3.

So every Loops join crossfades:
- **At each cut's first frame** within a Loops block, and at the first frame of the block after a Loops block, the outgoing cut plays on as a tail. The output fades linearly from the tail to the incoming cut over 2 ms (`⌈0.002 · rate⌉` frames). It starts wholly on the tail.
- **The tail** is `LivePlayer::Tail`: the outgoing cut's source, read position and stereo matrix. It reads on past the cut's end into audio already recorded.
  - At the end of the track pass the tail is the input itself.
  - At the end of a repeat it is the audio just after the span it looped.
- **A join with nothing to fade is skipped.** Where the incoming cut continues the outgoing one, as a track cut after a track cut does, the join is left alone, so the track stays the input bit for bit.
- **The crossfade sits in the preroll.** Every repeat starts 2 ms before its hit, so the fade-in is over by the time the hit's attack plays. The hit is untouched, and the join falls in the quietest part of the loop.
- Measured with the prototype on the held bass: the largest step falls from 0.598 to **0.016** (2.5 times the bass's own). On drums the level moves by at most 0.04 dB.
- **Reusing Rush's.** Rush's review fixes may add the same 2 ms join crossfade to Rush's rolls. Loops then shares that mechanism, its gate widened from Rush plans to Rush and Loops plans, rather than adding a second one.

### Every frame until the next block

As Rush found, a block laid out in samples can run out a frame before the next block activates on the grid. A Loops block that runs out plays its last cut on, as the same tail. So the join crossfade continues from the run-on with no gap.
- Loops blocks count elapsed time exactly through tempo changes (`LivePlayer::setExactRetime`), as Rush's and Tape's do.
- At tie tempos a Loops track block reads from the frame it activates on, as Rush's fix does for Rush. So the track pass is the input, sample for sample.

### Accent and transforms

Loops plans take no accent and no transform, whatever the Classic settings say (Rush's Finding 4). Their reads are the input, or the snapped capture. Only the level hold scales them.

### Restarts, switches and transport jumps (our call)

- **A restart that cuts off a Loops block** lets its cut play on as a tail, crossfading to what follows over 5 ms, as Rush's and Tape's restarts do. Examples are a program switch, an X zone change and the Classic surface's procedure arriving.
  - This includes a restart that lands exactly on a Loops block boundary, where the block has played out. The tail is then the last cut's continuation.
  - Beats are pass boundaries for every loop of a beat or longer, and switches land on beats. Rush's review measured that a boundary with no tail clicks on 22 to 37% of switches (Finding 2).
- **A restart into Loops** (a switch from another program) takes a tail of the outgoing Classic-player cut the same way. So a switch into Loops fades too. Only restarts into Loops take it, so no other switch changes.
- **A transport jump** (a seek, an arrangement loop's wrap, a stop) resets the rendering. A Loops join tail survives that reset when the next rendered frame follows the last. The output then crossfades from the loop's continuation into whatever the new position plays, rather than splicing.
  - This is how Tape's silent frame survives an arrangement loop (Tape's Finding 4).
  - The tail reads history, which a reset of the rendering keeps.

## Pad

- `isAvailable (loops)` is true. After this, every program is.
- Captions: X "Loop", Y "Depth" **(our call)**.
- **Mapping, with x and y in [0, 1]:**
  - procedure `loops`, 16 units per bar, seed 707 (unused: Loops draws nothing);
  - `zones.x = padZone (x, 3, previous.x)`, and `loopBars = {1, ½, ¼}[zones.x]`. Left is a bar, right is a quarter bar;
  - `loopDepth = min (⌊4y⌋, 3)`: four equal steps, with no hysteresis;
  - the base's other `CutParameters`. Straight comes from the pad's switch, as for every program, and has no effect.
- **X is a discrete zone,** with the pad's 3% hysteresis. Changing it restarts the cutting at the next unit boundary, as for Cut and Glitch. A new loop length is a new phrase with a new capture.
- **Y is not a zone.** Moving it never restarts. The new depth takes effect from the next pass.
- The pad page gives Loops a tooltip in the voice of the others. The README gets one sentence, and its "with Loops to follow" goes.
- **No program is unavailable any more.**
  - The editor's "Coming in a later update" tooltip, its "… ISN'T AVAILABLE YET" headline and the processor's `padUnavailable` display flag have nothing left to show, so they go.
  - `isAvailable` stays. It is the engine's precondition for `mapPad`, and `PadGate`'s guard against a program value outside the eight.
- The curves are starting points, tuned by ear. The verdict goes in the listening audit.

## Known limits

- **The first repeat is a pass away.** Loops is forward: the track plays out the pass you pressed in, and the first repeat comes at the next loop boundary. That is at most a bar with X at the left.
- **The capture reaches back, including after a restart.** Moving X or switching into Loops captures the pass the restart lands in. The part of it before the restart is track audio the listener didn't hear. The repeat then sounds like the loop jumping to where the track is now.
- **Late in the phrase, no repeat.** With shorter loops, a press in the last pass of a phrase plays the track until the next phrase.
- **Dense mixes mostly start on the grid.** The detector marks few hits over a sustained bass: one eighth in four on the review's loop. A loop then starts 2 ms before its grid point, which is right for music on the grid and up to the music's own timing off it.
- **The level hold measures RMS,** not loudness. A deep level of a kick is held to the loop's RMS, so its hits are softer than the loop's kick: up to 7.7 dB on drums alone. This is the by-ear risk most worth checking.
- **Tempo changes don't stretch the loop.** A repeat reads at speed 1. Faster, it plays the start of the capture; slower, it runs on into the audio after it. The join crossfade hides the seam. A meter change mid-phrase is the same.
- **The track's next hit can leak.** At the end of the track pass the tail is the track itself, so its next hit, if exactly on the grid, can sound for under 2 ms under the fade before the repeat's hit, which arrives 2 ms later.
- **Short loops of a held bass note** at the deepest level (an eighth of a beat loop is 62.5 ms at 120 bpm) are a pulse in the bass. The joins are crossfaded, so they don't click, but it is a pulse.
- **Bars of 16ths.** In meters whose bar isn't a whole number of eighths (9/16, 7/16), the deepest level is off the 64th grid. Straight doesn't change it.
- **In 3/4 and other meters, X's steps are halves of the bar.** In 3/4 they are 3, 1½ and ¾ beats, not two beats and one.
- **Mix below 100%** layers the loop over the track it was taken from, two moments at once.
- **At 44.1 kHz, a restart on an odd 16th can land a sample apart, depending on the host's buffer size.** This predates Loops, as Filter's and Rush's specs record. The capture is computed from where the restart lands. Passes start on whole units.
