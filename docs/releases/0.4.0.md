# Livecut 0.4.0 — Perform

Audio Hold keeps a complete wet phrase playing while the cutting engine continues
behind it. Swing shifts paired eighth-note timing, and Accent adds deterministic
variation to cut levels. Their neutral defaults preserve the 0.3 sound exactly.

## Performing

**Audio Hold** can be requested before audio is ready. It waits for a fresh whole
natural phrase, then enters on a bar line. With a phrase already ready, it enters
on the next bar. Switching Hold off schedules release for the next bar too. The
status line distinguishes live input, waiting for a phrase, pending entry, held
audio and pending release.

Hold records wet audio after the cut effects and Accent, before global Wet and
Trim. The global mixer runs once: dry remains the current live input, and Wet
blends that input with the held phrase. Final-output Capture and WAV export still
record the post-mix result, including Hold if it is playing.

Held audio follows musical time by **varispeed**. Tempo changes change playback
speed and pitch together; this is not pitch-preserving time-stretching. A 5 ms
wet-lane transition handles entry/release, and short 2 ms loop-end fades soften
seams. The wet cache freezes during Hold; releasing rearms recording at the next
full natural phrase start after the transition. No new wet phrase is promised
while held. An existing complete wet phrase can be reused until a fresh one lands.

Stopping, seeking, looping to a discontinuous host position or changing meter
immediately invalidates held audio and wet readiness. If Hold remains requested,
it waits visibly for a fresh complete phrase. Starting in the middle of a phrase
never publishes that partial phrase as complete. Audio is transient and is not
saved in DAW project state; recalling a saved Hold request cannot restore PCM.

**Swing** ranges from 50% (straight) to 75%. Each complete quarter note contains
two eighths: its middle eighth moves from 50% toward 75%, and its endpoints stay
fixed. An unmatched final eighth in an odd meter remains straight. Sub-quarter
bars remain straight. Changes land on the next bar, restarting that bar's block
under the existing structural-change policy. With Swing active, meter changes
clear pending onsets and resume in the new meter; neutral mode keeps the 0.3 path.

**Accent** adds attenuation-only cut-level variation, from none to a maximum 25%
reduction. It is deterministic for the seed, phrase bar and phrase-wide cut
ordinal. It does not draw from the pattern generator or move cut endpoints.
The effects can still alter the resulting output level as usual.

## Sources, timing and bounds

One raw stereo history replaces the player's old per-block recording arrays.
Every plan stamps an absolute source frame before that frame is ingested.
Planning follows straight musical boundaries; activation and all internal cut
endpoints follow Swing's mapped musical positions. Source origins never move
with Swing, and tempo changes retime remaining endpoints from actual musical
progress instead of reinterpreting earlier elapsed samples at the latest tempo.

The declared source extent stays at the classic eight seconds. The history
retains **64 seconds plus two interpolation guards**, because a block can replay
that first eight seconds throughout an eight-bar phrase. Every supported bar is
at most eight seconds; blocks end inside their phrase and Swing preserves bar
endpoints. This bounds the lifetime of the earliest source frame. Long multi-bar
repeats are tested explicitly; an eight-second rolling ring would lose them.

A fixed queue owns 16 block plans, including their cuts, source identity and the
six existing randomized effect settings. Activation draws no random values. At
maximum positive Swing the delay is at most a quarter beat. Dense one-unit plans
at 32 units per bar need at most nine queued starts in bars with complete quarter
pairs; fractional endings are straight, and the other procedures have a bounded
recovery/fill margin. Queue exhaustion or unavailable source audio is a visible
engine error requiring transport restart, rather than silently selecting other
audio or dropping a plan.

Hold and final-output Capture each prepare two stereo buffers, bounded to
12,288,000 frames per slot. They hold 64 seconds at rates through 192 kHz; above
that rate their available duration is shorter. An oversized wet phrase is
rejected in full and cannot enter Hold. Final-output Capture retains its 0.3
explicit capacity/busy behavior. File-worker leases never own the Hold cache.

Approximate base PCM memory (decimal MB), excluding effect storage, small plan
and player arrays, worker stacks and allocator overhead:

| Processing rate | Raw history | Wet Hold cache | Final Capture buffers | Base total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 48 kHz | 24.6 MB | 49.2 MB | 49.2 MB | 122.9 MB |
| 96 kHz | 49.2 MB | 98.3 MB | 98.3 MB | 245.8 MB |
| 192 kHz | 98.3 MB | 196.6 MB | 196.6 MB | 491.5 MB |

A captured export snapshot adds up to another 24.6 / 49.2 / 98.3 MB respectively.
Replacing it briefly retains both old and new snapshots: full capture peaks are
approximately 172.0 / 344.1 / 688.1 MB. Sample-rate preparation can temporarily
retain old leased storage or reallocation buffers beyond those figures. Raw
history remains a full 64 seconds even above 192 kHz, so higher-rate memory should
be calculated from the actual configured rate and capped capture durations.

No audio callback allocation, lock, worker wait or complete-phrase copy is needed
for Hold, source playback, planning or groove controls. Because held audio can
continue indefinitely during a running host transport, the processor advertises
an infinite tail. JUCE maps it to VST3's infinite-tail flag; AU forwards the tail
time. Host/format validation is tracked separately in the validation record.

## Compatibility and scope

Swing, Accent and Audio Hold are appended after all existing parameters with AU
version hint 4. Saved state uses `schemaVersion=4`, while `algorithmVersion=2`
continues to identify the unchanged legacy pattern algorithms. Missing controls
restore 50% Swing, zero Accent and Hold off, including on an adjusted instance.

A test-only 0.3 Cutter/player oracle is preserved from commit
`f054b36e232ecd20f1986d5856f42bdcf96bc9bf`. Same-process comparisons preserve exact
float behavior across all four procedures, pitched cuts, pan, fades, crusher,
feedback comb, tempo changes and slow multi-bar repeats. Shared cut procedures
and effects remain unchanged so that reference stays meaningful.

No look-ahead, reverse playback, onset analysis, file drag-out or time-stretch
library is included in this release. See `0.4.0-validation.md` for test evidence
and the limits of local validation.
